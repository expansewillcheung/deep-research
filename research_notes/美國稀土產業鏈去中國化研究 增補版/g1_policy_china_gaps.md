## 2. DFARS 252.225-7052 (1 Jan 2027 magnet expansion): DoD delay, deviation, waiver; Senate FY2027 NDAA vs House Sec. 1801

### Takeaway
No DoD delay rule, class deviation or waiver postponing 1 Jan 2027 was found; instead the White House tightened it via EO 14415 (20 Jul 2026), which stops routine waivers from 1 Jan 2027. Senate FY2027 NDAA magnet provisions and the content of House "Sec. 1801" could not be found.

### Cited Findings
- Clause bars SmCo and NdFeB magnets, tantalum, tungsten powders/heavy alloys with any stage (mining through fabrication) in China, Russia, Iran, North Korea from 1 Jan 2027; statutory basis is Sec. 854 of P.L. 118-31 (FY2024 NDAA) — [Metal-Powder.tech](https://www.metal-powder.tech/?p=283825) (secondary); Federal Register source on the 2024 rule: [govinfo FR 30 May 2024](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-05-30/pdf/2024-11516.pdf)
- Defense contractors lobbied the administration to postpone the 1 Jan 2027 enforcement, reported by FT in mid-May 2026 and again in a June 2026 newsletter — [Adamas Intelligence](https://www.adamasintel.com/?p=16856); [X-Risk Daily newsletter](https://buttondown.com/x-risk-daily/archive/us-defence-companies-lobby-to-delay-ban-on/)
- EO 14415, signed 20 July 2026, directs the Secretary of War and service secretaries to cease issuing waivers under 10 U.S.C. 4872(c)(1) for covered materials from covered countries effective 1 Jan 2027; waiver authority is not repealed but requires a DoD-accepted mitigation plan (source identification, exhaustive sourcing effort, removal timeline). Covered materials per Orrick/other reporting include magnets, tantalum, tungsten, and (per a second source) molybdenum — [Orrick, Sept 2026](https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2026/09/Defense-Supply-Chain-Compliance-Critical-Minerals-Waiver-Cessation-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027)
- Within 180 days of EO 14415 (about mid-Jan 2027) DoD must require primes and subs at any tier to submit an indentured bill of materials traced to raw-material origin and adopt supplier-vetting procedures — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2026/09/Defense-Supply-Chain-Compliance-Critical-Minerals-Waiver-Cessation-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027)
- The Orrick page mentions no class deviation, implementing guidance or delay of 1 Jan 2027 — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2026/09/Defense-Supply-Chain-Compliance-Critical-Minerals-Waiver-Cessation-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027)
- FY2026 NDAA (signed Dec 2025) expanded restricted minerals (per Orrick: molybdenum, gallium, germanium among additions) with phased timelines; battery restrictions effective 1 Jan 2028 / 2029 / 30 Jan 2031 — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/Insights/2026/09/Defense-Supply-Chain-Compliance-Critical-Minerals-Waiver-Cessation-Takes-Effect-January-1-2027)
- Oct 2026 commentary still treats 1 Jan 2027 as operative — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/?p=191317); [Oregon Group](https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/rare-earths/us-suppliers-not-ready-for-pentagons-2027-ban-on-china-sourced-magnets-and-minerals/) (supplier readiness doubts; NdFeB US demand ~48,000 t in 2025 vs ~300 t domestic, reported there)
- House FY2027 NDAA is H.R. 8800 (HASC markup 4 June 2026 per docs.house.gov record). A Finstad amendment amends "Sec. 1804" to add non-rare-earth permanent magnets; no "Sec. 1801" text retrieved — [House Armed Services amendment record](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/AS/AS00/20260604/119333/BILLS-119-HR8800-R000575-Amdt-CHMEB1.pdf)
- A Tech Times piece (6 Oct 2026) says critical-mineral bills (e.g., H.R. 4090 passed House 224-195, pending in Senate ENR) are "candidates for inclusion in the NDAA"; it gives no NDAA magnet text — [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328654/20261006/congress-moves-break-china-rare-earth-grip-before-november-deadline.htm) (low-grade source)
- A separate aihorizonnetwork timeline (low-grade, secondary) lists Defense Production Act authority as expiring 11 Dec 2026 after a 10-week stopgap extension, with H.R. 7688 (5-year extension) not passed — [AI Horizon Network](https://www.aihorizonnetwork.com/blog/china-rare-earth-export-controls-timeline)

### Inferences

## 3. Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and tariff stack on Chinese NdFeB magnets

### Takeaway
The Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on 20 Feb 2026 (reported 6-3). A Section 122 global tariff (10%, from 24 Feb) expired 24 July 2026 and was largely replaced by Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs (China 12.5%) from 24 July 2026. A magnet-specific combined rate was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Ruling 20 Feb 2026: IEEPA does not authorize tariffs; vote 6-3 (per Business Sweden) — [Business Sweden](https://www.business-sweden.com/insights/articles/us-supreme-court-blocks-ieepa-tariffs-as-the-administration-introduces-alternative-measure/); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/supreme-court-holds-ieepa-tariffs-unlawful-president-trump-terminates-and-partially)
- Section 122 10% global tariff effective 24 Feb 2026 (a rise to 15% was directed but sources conflict on whether implemented) — [Cozen](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2026/supreme-court-rules-president-lacks-ieepa-tariff-authority-president-imposes-section-122-tariffs); [Zonos](https://zonos.com/ieepa-tariff-ruling-updates)
- CIT held Section 122 tariffs unlawful on 7 May 2026 (narrow; stayed by Federal Circuit pending appeal); Section 122 tariffs expired 24 July 2026 by statute (150-day limit) — [ABA Procurement Lawyer, Fall 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2026-fall/ieepa-tariffs-struck-down-replacement-tariffs-expired/) (via search summary; page returned 403)
- Replacement: Section 301 forced-labor duties of 10% or 12.5% on 60 economies, effective 24 July 2026 12:01 a.m. ET; China at 12.5%; USTR added 471 HTS subheadings (largely raw materials/supply-chain-critical goods) to exemptions after comment; may stack with existing Section 301 China tariffs; a separate overcapacity 301 investigation is pending — [Trivium China, 24 Jul 2026](https://triviumchina.com/?p=206359); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/06/ustr-announces-findings-and-calls-for-comments-in-section-301-forced-labor-investigation); [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/section-301-duties-for-forced-labor-step-3-in-ieepa-replacement-and-its-impact/pdf/)
- Section 232 tariffs (steel, aluminum, copper, lumber) unaffected by the ruling — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2026-fall/ieepa-tariffs-struck-down-replacement-tariffs-expired/)

### Inferences
- Pre-existing Section 301 China tariff on NdFeB magnets (HTS 8505.11) is a separate historical layer not re-verified here; the writer should flag that existing 301 rate (previously reported 25% on List 3; scheduled increase to 25%/higher in 2026 for permanent magnets under Biden-era 301 four-year review) as unverified in this pass.

## 6. 45X critical minerals credit phase-out end year under OBBBA

### Takeaway
Under P.L. 119-21 (OBBBA), the 45X critical minerals credit phases down to 75% (2031), 50% (2032), 25% (2033) and is zero for minerals produced in 2034 and later. So 2033 is the last year with any credit; the credit "ends" in 2034. I did not retrieve the statute text itself.

### Cited Findings
- 100% through 2030; 75% in 2031; 50% in 2032; 25% in 2033; credit expires in 2034 (metallurgical coal added, eligible only through 2029) — [CRS In Focus IF12809 (Aug 2026)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-08-13_IF12809_6951a5de81763ce42e359ec101d527e70bf9ff16.html); [Kirkland & Ellis, Aug 2025](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/08/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-brings-big-changes-to-green-energy-tax-credits)
- Final law moved phase-out dates later than the House 22 May 2025 version — [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/critical-minerals-funding-obb/)

### Inferences
- Phrase "phase-out ends 2033" is correct only as "last partial-credit year"; "credit eliminated from 2034" is the accurate framing.

### Gaps
- Statute text (IRC 45X(b)(3)(B) as amended by OBBBA Sec. 70514) not directly read; CRS and law-firm summaries agree.
