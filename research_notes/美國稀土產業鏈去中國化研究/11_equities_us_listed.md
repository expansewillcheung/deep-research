## Q1. Market snapshot: price, performance, valuation and sentiment across the group

### Takeaway
After a 2025 surge, the whole US rare earth group sold off hard through 2026. Most names are 50–80% below their 52-week highs. MP is down 22% YTD, USAR -14%, UUUU -44%, NB -41%, METC -60%, AREC -56% and REMX -28%. The press attributes the selling to sector-wide de-rating on US–China "thaw" hopes, which erode the scarcity premium, and to AI-trade risk-off episodes, not to company news. Sell-side targets have not followed prices down: consensus targets imply 80–250% upside. That gap points to a sentiment-driven reset, not cuts to fundamentals.

### Inferences
- Producers with revenue (MP, Lynas) have fallen less YTD than pre-revenue builders (USAR, CRML on its 52-week peak, METC, AREC). This matches the "builders vs producers" split some analysts describe.
- High short interest in USAR, CRML and METC, combined with targets far above price, implies very wide outcome distributions and the potential for sharp squeezes on policy headlines.
- MP's 52-week high of $76.80 excludes its October 2025 peak (about $100.25 intraday, monthly bar). Measured from that all-time peak, MP is down about 60%.

## Q2. Company profiles: business, assets, financials, valuation, government backing, catalysts and risks

### Takeaway
Only MP (and non-US Lynas) is a scaled, EBITDA-positive producer. MP's Q2-2026 adjusted EBITDA was $28.5M, helped by $17.6M of US Department of War price-protection income, and it holds $1.45B of cash. USAR and UUUU are heavily funded consolidators: USAR held $1.53B of cash after a $1.5B PIPE and bought Serra Verde for about $2.8B; UUUU holds about $937M of liquidity and bought ASM and VAC. Both carry major integration and dilution risk. NB, CRML, METC (Brook), REEMF and AREC are financing- or exploration-stage options.

### Inferences
**Tiering**
- **CORE:** MP and Lynas (ADR). These have revenue, positive adjusted EBITDA and government-anchored economics. MP's PPA price floor cushions it against a Chinese price flood.
- **CORE-SPECULATIVE:** UUUU. Its liquidity is about $937M and uranium cash flow is real, but the VAC/ASM integration and the share price collapse relative to the VAC deal price raise dilution risk.
- **SPECULATIVE:** USAR (very well funded but pre-magnet-revenue), NB (binary on EXIM), CRML (going concern), REEMF and IDR (small options).
- **AVOID or wait for a catalyst:** METC, given coal losses, litigation and an exploratory Brook deposit, and AREC, given its deconsolidated, micro-cap, high-short-interest profile.

**Price floor and MP's earnings**
- MP's PPA income ($59.9M in H1) is nearly equal to its H1 adjusted EBITDA ($65.1M). Without the floor, MP would roughly break even at the EBITDA level. The floor is the key asset if China floods the market, and the main risk if policy reverses.

**Policy sensitivity**
- If US–China détente continues, expect continued de-rating of the builders (USAR, CRML, METC), while floor-protected MP is relatively insulated.
- Re-escalation, such as new Chinese export controls, has historically produced the sharpest rallies (2025 precedent).

**Ownership and financing structure**
- USAR, UUUU and MP have all migrated toward government-anchored capital: CHIPS loans for USAR, an OSC loan for UUUU, and DoW preferred plus a price floor for MP.
- For the developers (NB, Round Top, Tanbreez), EXIM and other federal debt is the gating factor for construction.
