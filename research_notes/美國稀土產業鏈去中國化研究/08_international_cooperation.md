Note on method: about 25 search and fetch calls. Several primary government pages (state.gov, au.usembassy.gov, METI PDF, S&P PDF) returned HTTP 403, so some primary content comes through search-result summaries of those pages, or through secondary reporting that cites them. ## 1. Bilateral deals: what exists, what is funded, what is built

### Takeaway
Since Oct 2025 the US has built a dense web of bilateral critical-minerals frameworks: Australia, Japan, Malaysia, Thailand, Saudi Arabia, the Central Asian states, Ukraine, and 11 more MOUs signed at the Feb 2026 ministerial. Execution is very uneven. Australia is the only partner where US-backed rare earth projects have reached construction (Arafura Nolans broke ground 8 Oct 2026). Brazil's Serra Verde is the only operating non-Asian producer of all four magnet REEs to get closed US money (a $565M DFC loan, plus a $750M DoW commitment to an offtake SPV). Most other US "commitments" are non-binding EXIM/DFC letters of interest, roughly $4B in EXIM LOIs alone.

### Inferences
- The ratio of executed to announced money is low outside Australia and Brazil. Of the roughly US$4B in EXIM LOIs, little appears to have converted to final loans by Oct 2026. Actual allied US-backed REE capacity in construction is essentially Arafura Nolans, plus Iluka Eneabba (Australian-funded) and Serra Verde (operating, being upgraded).
- Most of the new money targets mining and early processing. Allied separation of heavy REEs (Dy/Tb) still depends on a handful of plants: Lynas Malaysia, Neo Estonia, Solvay, Caremag, and Iluka from 2027.

## 2. Multilateral architecture: MSP to FORGE, Pax Silica, price floors, G7, EU, Quad

### Takeaway
In February 2026 the US replaced the Minerals Security Partnership with FORGE and proposed a preferential trade zone with border-adjusted price floors. Pax Silica, a narrower US-led alliance that also covers semiconductors and AI, is the reported vehicle for those floors. By October 2026 the price-floor bloc is still being negotiated. The only operating price floors are bilateral US$110/kg NdPr arrangements (MP Materials, and reportedly Lynas). The EU has declined to back a price floor and instead pursues its own stockpile and joint-purchase centre (RESourceEU).

### Cited Findings
- On 4 Feb 2026 the Critical Minerals Ministerial in Washington drew 54 nations plus the EU. Vice President Vance proposed a preferential trade zone with coordinated price floors enforced through adjustable tariffs. — [CNBC, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/05/us-allies-critical-minerals-price-floors-forge-china-rare-earths-ai-chips-pax-silicchina-.html); [State Dept](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/02/2026-critical-minerals-ministerial)
- Secretary Rubio announced FORGE as the successor to the MSP, with a mandate to establish price protections and floors. Eleven new bilateral frameworks or MOUs were signed, including with Argentina, the Cook Islands, Ecuador, Guinea, Morocco, Paraguay, Peru, the Philippines, the UAE and Uzbekistan. — [State Dept fact sheet via AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602050532.html); [Conference Board](https://www.conference-board.org/pdfdownload.cfm?masterProductID=86893)
- Bloomberg reports that US agencies built the price-floor system and that it will roll out through Pax Silica. Members named include Australia, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, the UK and the UAE, with India expected to join. — [MINING.COM/Bloomberg](https://www.mining.com/web/us-agencies-have-developed-critical-minerals-price-floor-system/); [The Diplomat, Feb 2026](https://thediplomat.com/2026/02/pax-silica-and-the-china-us-critical-minerals-competition/)
- Skepticism: ORF America questions whether price floors are workable across the full range of REEs, and says the strategy is "unstructured." — [ORF America](https://orfamerica.org/orf-america-comments/an-unstructured-us-critical-mineral-strategy-takes-shape)
- Existing bilateral floors: a US$110/kg NdPr floor for MP Materials for 10 years (DoD deal, July 2025), and a US government commitment to buy Lynas oxides at a US$110/kg floor. — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later) (the Lynas floor is reported by CSIS; it was not confirmed in Lynas ASX filings here)
- Section 232: Proclamation 11001 (14 Jan 2026) imposed no immediate tariff on processed critical minerals. It ordered 180 days of negotiations with partners, with a report due about 13 Jul 2026. It cites possible "minimum import prices" and the deals with Australia, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Thailand and Japan. — [White House fact sheet, Jan 2026](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/01/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-directs-negotiations-to-adjust-imports-of-processed-critical-minerals-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states/); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/trump-administration-announces-results-of-critical-minerals-investigation-under-section-232-and-directs-us-officials-to-initiate-negotiations)
- G7 Évian summit (15–17 Jun 2026): leaders issued a declaration on critical mineral value chains covering processing and recycling, financing, and "standards-based approaches and revenue stabilisation." Canada's Critical Minerals Resilience and Production Alliance announced 13 new partnerships unlocking more than C$5B. — [Élysée](https://www.elysee.fr/en/G7evian/2026/06/17/the-outcomes-of-the-evian-g7-summit); [PM of Canada](https://www.pm.gc.ca/en/news/news-releases/2026/06/17/prime-minister-carney-secures-new-partnerships-defence-and-critical); [Dilichance](https://www.dilichance.eu/en/news-overview/g7-leaders-commit-to-securing-and-diversifying-critical-mineral-supply-chains)
- Seraph reports that Évian added stockpile coordination, but "a price-floor scheme found no European backing." — [Seraph](https://seraph.com/articles/europe-has-the-deposits-china-has-the-industry/)
- The EU adopted the RESourceEU Action Plan on 3 Dec 2025. It created a European Critical Raw Materials Centre for joint purchasing and stockpiling. A pilot stockpile has run since early 2026, with tungsten, rare earths and gallium shortlisted. — [Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2025/12/resourceeu-action-plan-strengthening-the-eus-access-to-critical-raw-materials/); [Mining Digital](https://miningdigital.com/news/what-europes-minerals-stockpile-means-for-mining)
- In 2026 the EU added four rare earth Strategic Projects, including Solvay's BREES expansion at La Rochelle. — [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/eu-adds-four-rare-earth-projects-to-strategic-list-to-support-recycling-processing-goals/)
- A G7 critical minerals ministerial was held in Washington on 12 Jan 2026, and India's minister Vaishnaw attended. — [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/union-minister-ashwini-vaishnaw-to-participate-in-g7-critical-minerals-ministerial-meeting-today-in-washington)

### Inferences
- Allied coordination is splitting into a US track (Pax Silica/FORGE with price floors backed by tariffs) and an EU track (stockpile and joint purchasing, no floor). Japan sits in both. A single allied price mechanism by end-2026 looks unlikely.
- Without a multilateral floor, the US is carrying price risk bilaterally (MP, Lynas, the Serra Verde SPV). That limits how many allied projects can be underwritten.

### Gaps
- The outcome of the 30-day US–EU–Japan price-floor talks (from Feb 2026) and of the July 2026 Section 232 report were not found.
- No Quad critical minerals deliverables for 2026 were found.
- No full membership list or legal text for Pax Silica was found.

## 3. Key allied producers and their capacity

### Takeaway
Lynas is still the only large allied separator: 7,260 t NdPr in FY26, plus first commercial Dy/Tb from Malaysia since mid-2025. The next allied wave falls mostly in 2026–2027: Iluka Eneabba (mid-2027), Arafura Nolans (construction began Oct 2026), Caremag France (late 2026), SRC (2027), Serra Verde expansion (6,500 t TREO by 2027), Solvay La Rochelle expansion, and Neo Narva magnets (2,000 t/yr). Japan remains the main allied magnet maker, at about 15% of the global magnet market.

### Cited Findings
- Lynas produced 7,260 t NdPr in FY26 (to 30 Jun 2026), up 11%. The June 2026 quarter produced 1,857 t NdPr and 3,481 t total REO. ## 4. How much allied non-China NdPr and Dy/Tb exists (2025) and by 2030

### Takeaway
China still supplies about 84% of global NdPr oxide (S&P) and more than 90% of separated heavy REEs. Forecasts for 2030 cut China's NdPr share to about 69% (Bloomberg Intelligence), with about 44 kt/yr of new ex-China refining capacity possible. Ex-China output, however, is projected to lag ex-China demand by about 25 kt by 2028. For Dy/Tb, the most cautious sources expect China to keep more than 90% through 2030; optimistic ones put non-China Dy at 15–20%.

### Cited Findings
- S&P Global (8 May 2026): China holds 84% of global NdPr oxide supply. More than 20 projects could add about 44 kt of ex-China NdPr refining capacity by 2030. Ex-China production is expected to sit at about 27 kt, roughly 25 kt below ex-China demand by 2028, with average utilization around 74% through 2030. — [S&P Global NdPr Market Report](https://www.spglobal.com/content/dam/spglobal/global-assets/en/special-reports/critical-mineral-market-reports/Market%20Report%20-%20NdPr%20-%2008052026.pdf) (via search summary; direct fetch 403. The exact meaning of the "27 kt" figure, whether capacity or output and in which year, could not be verified from the PDF)
- Bloomberg Intelligence: China's NdPr share falls from about 90% (2024) to 69% (2030), but a global NdPr shortfall of up to 36% by 2030 remains possible. — [Rare Earth Exchanges summarizing BI](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/beyond-beijing-the-uneasy-rise-of-ex-china-rare-earth-supply-chains/)
- Adamas Intelligence (via a secondary summary): the NdPr oxide-equivalent shortfall grows from 21 kt/yr in 2030 to 68 kt/yr in 2035. — [Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/rare-earth-supply-gap-investors-september-2026/) (secondary; unverified)
- Heavy REEs: China controls more than 91% of global HREE supply and is expected to keep more than 90% through 2030. Non-China heavy separation may stay under 20% of global capacity by 2028 even if projects deliver. — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/beyond-beijing-the-uneasy-rise-of-ex-china-rare-earth-supply-chains/); contrasted by [Future Market Insights](https://www.futuremarketinsights.com/reports/dysprosium-market) (non-China Dy below 5% in 2024, rising to 15–20% by 2030; market-research firm, lower reliability)
- Myanmar (Kachin) supplied an estimated 57% of global Tb/Dy feed in 2023 (Adamas, cited by EarthRights). About two-thirds of the Tb/Dy processed in China comes from Myanmar. That ore flows almost entirely into Yunnan, so it is not an allied source. — [EarthRights, Apr 2025](https://earthrights.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Rare-Earth-Report-April-2025.pdf); [D+C](https://www.dandc.eu/en/article/myanmar-rare-earths-kachin-china)
- On 15 Aug 2026 the KIO rejected separate talks with the junta. Kachin supply remains a volatility risk to China's own heavy-REE feed. — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/kachin-rare-earth-supply-risk/) (trade site)

### Inferences
- Allied, non-China separated NdPr in 2025 was likely about 10–15% of global supply. This is consistent with S&P's 84% China share and Lynas at about 7 kt. Even on optimistic assumptions it rises to roughly 25–30% by 2030, short of ex-China demand. Friend-shoring therefore narrows but does not close the NdPr gap before 2030.
- Heavy REEs are the binding constraint. Allied Dy/Tb probably stays in the low hundreds of tonnes per year until Caremag, Iluka (2027+), Lynas expansion and Serra Verde ionic clays scale. China and Myanmar keep more than 80–90% through 2030.

### Gaps
- No Benchmark Mineral Intelligence or IEA 2026 ex-China share forecast was retrieved.
- No consolidated 2025 allied NdPr tonnage was found from a primary source.
- Exact Dy/Tb tonnages from Lynas and Neo are undisclosed.

## 5. Frictions: competition among allies, tariffs, Chinese capital, Chinese countermeasures

### Takeaway
Friend-shoring faces five main frictions:
- China is coercing allies directly (Japan was cut off from Dy/Tb oxide Nov 2025–May 2026).
- China's October 2025 extraterritorial controls are only suspended to 10 Nov 2026, while the April 2025 licensing regime remains.
- Allies impose resource-nationalist rules, such as Malaysia's ban on raw REE exports.
- Chinese capital is buying allied-continent projects (Peak/Ngualla to Shenghe).
- Allies are diverging on market tools (the EU rejects price floors), and US Section 232 tariffs hang over partners.

### Cited Findings
- China's April 2025 licensing on seven medium and heavy REEs was never suspended. The October 2025 package added five elements, a 0.1% foreign-content rule, and an embargo on Chinese technology and personnel going to foreign projects. That package was suspended for one year, to about 10 Nov 2026. — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later); [Bloomberg, 7 Nov 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-07/china-formalizes-rare-earth-curbs-suspension-after-trade-truce); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/10/china-suspends-some-critical-mineral-export-curbs-to-the-us-as-trade-truce-takes-hold.html)
- The US–China truce has reportedly been extended to 10 Jan 2027. Sources conflict on whether the REE suspension was explicitly extended. — [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/us-and-china-extend-their-rare-earth-truce-by-just-two-months-to-january/); [Rinnovabili](https://www.rinnovabili.net/policy-and-affairs/environmental-policies/chinese-rare-earth-exports-trump-xi-truce/); [technology.org, 19 Oct 2026](https://technology.org/2026/10/19/china-rare-earth-export-pause-november-10)
- China's coercion of Japan:
  - On 6 Jan 2026 MOFCOM banned dual-use exports to Japan for military end-uses.
  - In February it suspended dual-use shipments to 20 Japanese firms (including MHI, KHI, IHI and NEC) and put 20 more on a watch list.
  - One source reports zero Chinese Dy/Tb oxide exports to Japan from Nov 2025 to May 2026, while finished magnets kept flowing.
  - China still supplies about 80% of Japan's REE imports.
  - — [UPI, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/06/22/rare-earth-magnet-exports-japan-fell/2481782173091/); [Investing News](https://investingnews.com/china-prohibits-japanese-ree-shipments/); [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/japan-rare-earth-china-restrictions/)
- After the November 2025 easing, Chinese magnet exports to Europe rose 60% year on year, while US imports fell 11%. Yttrium exports to the US collapsed: 17 t in Apr–Dec 2025, against 333 t in the prior eight months. — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- Malaysia's ban on raw REE exports was retained despite the US deal. — [MINING.COM/Reuters](https://www.mining.com/web/malaysias-ban-on-raw-rare-earths-exports-remains-despite-us-deal/)
- Chinese capital in allied-region projects: Shenghe acquired Peak Rare Earths (Ngualla, Tanzania), which makes the project ineligible for some Western support. Serra Verde (Brazil) previously sold to Chinese buyers before the US-linked restructuring. — [rare-earth-mining.com](https://rare-earth-mining.com/top-10-africa-rare-earth-projects/) (secondary); [MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/web/brazils-only-rare-earth-producer-offers-us-stake-option-for-loan-deal/)
- Allies compete for scarce feed: Solvay (France) has binding deals mainly with US magnet makers. Kangankunde (Malawi) feed goes to Australia's Iluka. EU and US stockpiles both target REEs. — [Seraph](https://seraph.com/articles/europe-has-the-deposits-china-has-the-industry/); [Africa Briefing](https://africabriefing.com/malawi-targets-2026-rare-earth-production/); [Mining Digital](https://miningdigital.com/news/what-europes-minerals-stockpile-means-for-mining)
- From 1 Jan 2027 the DoD bans REE materials and magnets sourced from China, Russia, Iran and North Korea (DFARS). This raises demand for allied supply ahead of its arrival. — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- Analysts observe G7 operational divergence on critical minerals, AI and trade at Évian. — [NOVUM](https://novummx.com/en/post/g7-evian-critical-minerals-bilateral-deals/)

### Inferences
- **Timeline effect of friend-shoring:** Allied supply plausibly lets the US cover most non-defense NdPr oxide needs from non-China sources by about 2028–2030. The basis is Lynas (about 7 kt), MP domestic output, and from 2027 Iluka, Nolans and Serra Verde. The constraint is competition with Japan, Korea and the EU, which also want the same tonnes.
- **Heavy REEs (Dy/Tb):** The de-sinicization timeline is still about 2030 or later. China's technology-export embargo slows allied plant construction, and the January 2027 DoD deadline will likely be met only through stockpiles, waivers or recycled feed.
- **Diplomacy-to-tonnes lag:** Framework signing in Oct 2025 led to first construction in Aug–Oct 2026 and first output in 2027–2028. Most announced bilateral deals (Central Asia, Ukraine, Greenland, Africa) will not produce separated REO before about 2029–2032.

### Gaps
- No confirmation of whether China reinstated or extended its October 2025 controls past 10 Nov 2026 (not yet happened as of 22 Oct).
- No information on tariff treatment of allied magnets (e.g., Japanese or Korean NdFeB) under any final Section 232 action.
- No data on Chinese equity in Australian or Canadian REE juniors after FIRB/ICA tightening.
