## Q1. Macro/sector backdrop: what is driving these equities in Oct 2026?

### Takeaway
Rare-earth equities ran up very strongly in 2025 to early/mid 2026 (REMX +89.5% in 2025, peak monthly close Apr 2026) and have since de-rated sharply (REMX −27.8% YTD, about −49% from the April close), because a US–China thaw eroded the "scarcity premium" and developers kept burning cash. The key near-term binary is China's 10 Nov 2026 expiry of its one-year suspension of the October-2025 expanded export controls. The April-2025 heavy-REE licensing regime stays in force either way.

### Inferences
- The sector trades as a geopolitical option on China's behaviour. A lapse or tightening on 10 Nov would likely re-rate the ex-China producers (Lynas, Iluka, Arafura) more than the Chinese names. An extension would likely extend the de-rating, with pre-revenue developers hit hardest.
- Price floors (MP's $110/kg, and Lynas's "price floor" contracts with Japanese/US customers) are turning ex-China NdPr into a separate, premium-priced market. That supports producer margins even if China spot prices soften.
- No reliable Oct-2026 NdPr/Dy/Tb spot price was found. Lynas's record A$98.2/kg basket ASP in the June 2026 quarter is the best proxy for the ex-China premium.

### Gaps
- October 2026 NdPr oxide, Dy and Tb prices (China domestic vs. ex-China/Rotterdam) were not found. Fastmarkets, SMM or Asian Metal would be needed.

## Q2. Allied producers/developers listed outside the US (Australia, Canada, Europe, Japan)

### Takeaway
Lynas is the only scaled, profitable ex-China separated light+heavy REE producer. It posted record FY26 results but trades ~39% lower y/y on operational hiccups, a CEO transition and the sector de-rating. Iluka (Eneabba, 2027) and Arafura (Nolans, first production 2029) are funded, government-backed builders, and their stocks are down 34–67% y/y. Australian Strategic Materials has been absorbed by Energy Fuels (US), the clearest example of US capital consolidating allied assets. Canadian names (Ucore, Aclara) are building US-sited separation plants that are still pre-revenue.

### Inferences
- **Summary positioning (my synthesis, not investment advice):**
  - *Quality producer:* Lynas. It has earnings, A$1.2B cash, heavy-REE capability, and US/Japan floor-priced contracts. Risks are ramp execution, the CEO vacancy, Malaysian politics, and a high trailing multiple.
  - *Funded builders:* Iluka (mineral-sands base plus Eneabba 2027) and Arafura (Nolans 2029, now with a Project Vault-linked offtake). Both have high beta to the 10 Nov outcome.
  - *Options:* Viridis, Meteoric, BRE, St George, NTU, Ucore, Aclara. These carry binary funding and offtake risk, and US-acquirer M&A is a key upside path (precedent: EFR–ASM).
  - *Adjacent large caps:* TDK and Shin-Etsu offer low-volatility, diluted exposure that outperformed in 2026.
  - *China hedge:* China Northern (quota/price leverage) and the JL Mag H-share (cheap valuation). They are correlated with the sector, so they are not a clean hedge.
- If China lets the suspension lapse or tightens controls on 10 Nov, the likely winners are Lynas, Arafura, Iluka and the heavy-REE developers. An extension would likely mean further de-rating for developers and relative resilience for Chinese producers on volume.
