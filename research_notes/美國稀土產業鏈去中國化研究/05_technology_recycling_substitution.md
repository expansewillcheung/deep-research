# Rare Earth Technology, R&D, Recycling and Substitution for the US Supply Chain (as of Oct 2026)

Notes compiled 2026-10-22. About 16 search/fetch calls. Most sources are company releases and trade press. TRL figures are the researcher's estimates (marked as inferences), not company-stated values unless noted. Items known only from background knowledge and not re-verified in this session are listed under Gaps, not presented as findings.

## 1. Separation innovations (RapidSX, ReElement chromatography, Phoenix Tailings, bio-based/lanmodulin, ionic liquids, membranes, electrochemical): readiness and cost vs solvent extraction

### Takeaway
None of the non-solvent-extraction separation technologies was running at commercial scale in the US as of October 2026. Ucore's RapidSX (a compact SX variant) and ReElement's chromatography are the closest: equipment is scaled up and DoD/DOE funding is committed, with first commercial output targeted for late 2026–2028. Phoenix Tailings has only preliminary commercial designs. Bio-based methods (lanmodulin) are still at lab or column scale. Cost advantages over conventional SX are claimed but not independently verified.

- LLNL has patented and demonstrated an all-aqueous REE extraction and separation scheme that uses immobilized lanmodulin protein. - LLNL offers a newer desorption technology that it describes as the best lanmodulin separation performance reported to date. — [LLNL IPO](https://ipo.llnl.gov/portfolios/life-sciences-biotech-healthcare/maximizing-rare-earth-element-separation-lanmodulin)
- Other recent research includes lanmodulin-decorated microbes (Advanced Materials, 2025) and lanmodulin for light REE separation from acidic leachate. — [Gut et al., Adv. Mater. 2025](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/adma.202412607); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398876935_Lanmodulin_as_protein-based_platform_for_the_separation_of_light_rare_earth_elements_from_acidic_leachate)
- No source found for a lanmodulin pilot plant or for a commercial-scale REEgen deployment.

### Inferences
- Researcher's TRL estimates: RapidSX TRL 7, moving to 8 once Machine A is commissioned. It has been demonstrated at pilot/demo scale (Kingston, Ontario), and its Louisiana commercial contactors are tested but not yet operating. ReElement chromatography TRL 6–7: it produces at small commercial volume, but tonnage claims at the >1,000 t scale are not demonstrated. Phoenix Tailings TRL 6, with pilot validation and preliminary commercial design. Lanmodulin/bio-separation TRL 3–4. Ionic liquids, membranes and electrochemical methods TRL 3–5 (not verified this session).
- RapidSX is still solvent extraction chemistry, made more compact with a smaller footprint, inventory and residence time. Its risk is in execution, not in the chemistry. Chromatography's economics at bulk scale are the main open question. China's SX cost base is the benchmark, and no US technology has published audited opex against it.
- The near-term US "shortcut" in separation is more likely from capital (DoD/DOE loans plus the $110/kg price floor) than from technology cost advantages. Without price floors or offtake, new US separation remains uncompetitive with Chinese SX on unit cost.

### Gaps
- No independent, third-party techno-economic comparison ($/kg) of RapidSX or chromatography against conventional SX. Company claims (e.g., Ucore's lower capex/opex, faster throughput) were not verified with figures in this session.
- ORNL ionic-liquid/ligand work (e.g., diglycolamide ligands), Ames Lab CMI membrane solvent extraction, and electrochemical separation: no 2026 sources retrieved. Status unverified.
- REEgen (a Cornell/Columbia bio-based startup): no 2026 sources found.
- Exact capacity (t/yr) of ReElement's current Indiana production was not found in primary sources.

## 2. Recycling (Cyclic Materials, Noveon, HyProMag USA, MP/Apple, Redwood, REEcycle, Ionic Rare Earths): status and volume potential through 2035

### Takeaway
US magnet recycling became commercial in 2026. Cyclic Materials' Mesa, Arizona plant opened in September 2026, Noveon's magnet-to-magnet line runs in Texas, and MP/Apple and HyProMag USA follow in 2027–28. Even so, recycling can realistically supply only about 10–15% of magnet demand by 2035. The IEA's larger figure (cutting primary supply needs by up to 35%) is for 2050. End-of-life collection is the binding constraint, and most current secondary supply is manufacturing scrap concentrated in China.

### Cited Findings
**Cyclic Materials**
- September 2026: Cyclic began commercial operation of its first US facility in Mesa, Arizona (>$20M investment). The plant handles about 25,000 t/yr of magnet-bearing scrap and e-waste, and more than 7,000 t had already been delivered at opening. Its MagCycle process mechanically extracts magnets and produces "Mag-Xtract", which goes to chemical partners for refining into REOs. It also recovers copper, aluminum and steel. — [Engineering News, Sept 10 2026](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/cyclic-materials-starts-commercial-operation-of-rare-earths-recovery-facility-in-arizona-2026-09-10); [Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/rare-earth-recycler-cyclic-materials-opens-first-us-facility-mesa-arizona/830028/); [Electrek, Sept 10 2026](https://electrek.co/2026/09/10/a-new-arizona-plant-is-going-after-the-rare-earth-supply-crunch)
- A second US campus in Chesterfield County, South Carolina, combining magnet recovery and REE refining, is planned for 2028. Cyclic also signed a July 2026 feedstock deal with ERI. — [Waste Dive](https://www.wastedive.com/news/rare-earth-recycler-cyclic-materials-opens-first-us-facility-mesa-arizona/830058/)
- Note: 25,000 t/yr is scrap throughput. The contained rare earth content is a small fraction of that and was not disclosed.

**Noveon Magnetics (San Marcos, Texas)**
- Noveon makes sintered NdFeB using its Magnet-to-Magnet (M2M) process, which recycles end-of-life magnets directly into new magnets without full chemical separation.
- January 6, 2026: Noveon, Kangwon Energy and LG Electronics launched a closed-loop program that recycles end-of-life magnets from LG products into new magnets for LG, with expansion at a South Korean JV facility. — [PR Newswire, Jan 6 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/noveon-magnetics-kangwon-energy-and-lg-electronics-launch-closed-loop-rare-earth-magnet-recycling-initiative-302654272.html)
- Solvay will supply REOs to Noveon from 2026. Noveon also has a reported deal to deliver 1,000 t of magnets to Nidec Motor over five years. — [Recycling Today](https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/noveon-magnetics-launches-rare-earth-magnet-recycling-initiative/); [Noveon news](https://noveon.co/news)

**MP Materials / Apple**
- July 15, 2025: Apple and MP signed a $500M long-term agreement for US-made magnets from 100% recycled material. MP will build a dedicated commercial-scale recycling line at Mountain Pass, after nearly five years of piloting, and make the magnets at Fort Worth ("Independence"). Shipments begin in 2027 and will scale to "hundreds of millions of Apple devices". The deal also covers joint R&D on new magnet materials. — [MP Materials release](https://mpmaterials.com/news/mp-materials-and-apple-announce-500-million-partnership-to-produce-recycled-rare-earth-magnets-in-the-united-states); [Apple via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250715345597/en/Apple-expands-U.S.-supply-chain-with-500-million-commitment-to-American-rare-earth-magnets)
- Apple says it has surpassed 99% use of recycled REEs in all magnets, per its Environmental Progress Report. — [ESG Today summary](https://www.esgtoday.com/apple-mp-materials-to-build-rare-earths-recycling-facility-under-new-500-million-supply-deal/)

**HyProMag USA (Dallas–Fort Worth "Texas Hub"; owned by Mkango/CoTec)**
- Uses HPMS (Hydrogen Processing of Magnet Scrap), licensed from the University of Birmingham.
- July 27, 2026: HyProMag USA brought forward magnet finishing, targeting H1 2027 to finish up to 20 t of magnet blocks from its UK and German operations. The HPMS recycling section has slipped to Q2 2028. A June 2026 update had targeted H2 2027 for commissioning the hub. — [Newswire, July 2026](https://www.newswire.com/news/hypromag-usa-to-establish-early-magnet-finishing-capability-as-part-of-22834665); [Barchart, June 2026](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/2578923/mkango-resources-limited-announces-hypromag-usa-advances-texas-hub-magnet-platform)
- Planned capacity: about 400 t/yr of recycled sintered NdFeB plus about 278 t of co-products in the initial phase, ramping toward about 1,526 t of magnetic products. The feasibility study cited 750 t/yr of recycled sintered magnets within five years. Financing and offtake are still under discussion. — [Resource Recycling, Dec 2025](https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/2025/12/12/hypromag-to-site-rare-earth-magnet-hub-in-texas/)

**Volume potential**
- IEA: recycling could lower the need for primary supply by up to 35% by 2050. Manufacturing scrap is currently the majority of secondary supply and is concentrated in China. End-of-life permanent magnet recycling remains low. — [IEA, Rare Earth Elements: Pathways to secure and diversified supply chains (PDF)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf); [IEA executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/rare-earth-elements/executive-summary)
- IEA: demand for magnet rare earths outside China rises about 50% by 2035, while planned ex-China capacity meets only about half of mining, a quarter of refining and under a fifth of magnet needs in 2035. — [IEA news](https://www.iea.org/news/new-projects-partnerships-and-policies-are-needed-to-address-supply-chain-risks-for-rare-earth-elements)
- A consultancy estimate (ESI, not the IEA) puts recycling at 8–15% of global REE magnet demand by 2030 and 12–15% by 2035. Functional REE recycling rates are below 1% today. — [Energy Solutions](https://energy-solutions.co/articles/sub/e-waste-rare-earth-recovery-economics-2035)
- A Korean study finds China-free supply, recycling included, could meet only 35–40% of 2035 magnet demand. — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/china-free-rare-earth-supply-could-meet-just-35-40-of-2035-magnet-demand-korean-study-warns/)

### Inferences
- Researcher's TRL estimates: mechanical magnet recovery (Cyclic) TRL 8–9, now commercial. Noveon M2M TRL 8, at commercial but modest volume. HPMS TRL 7, commercial in the UK/Germany but not yet in the US. MP's recycling line TRL 7–8, piloted for about five years with commercial output from 2027.
- Recycling is the fastest-deploying domestic lever, with plants built in 1–3 years versus 7–15 for mines. But volume is capped by the EV/wind end-of-life wave, which arrives mostly after 2035. Before 2035, feedstock comes mainly from manufacturing swarf, HDDs, consumer electronics and industrial motors. Apple's closed loop is meaningful for consumer electronics but small in tonnage.
- Direct magnet-to-magnet routes (Noveon, HPMS) skip separation and so partly bypass the US separation bottleneck. However, they cannot correct alloy composition freely (e.g., Dy content), so some recycled output still needs chemical re-separation (Cyclic's route via refiners).
- Rough planning number: recycling could meet about 10–15% of US magnet REE needs by 2035 if collection policy improves. This is not enough to replace primary supply.

### Gaps
- Redwood Materials: no evidence found of rare earth recovery. Its focus remains batteries (Li, Ni, Co, Cu).
- REEcycle (Texas) and Ionic Rare Earths (Ionic Technologies in Belfast; US partnerships): no 2026 sources retrieved.
- Contained REO/NdPr tonnage at Cyclic's Mesa plant and Noveon's current output (t/yr) not disclosed in retrieved sources.
- No US-specific recycling-share-of-demand projection from DOE/USGS found.

## 3. Reduction and substitution (Niron iron nitride, heavy-REE-free NdFeB, ferrite/induction/EESM motors, Tesla, BMW, Ce/La substitution, SmCo)

### Takeaway
Demand-side technologies are the most credible way to shorten US dependence on Dy/Tb and, to a lesser extent, Nd/Pr. BMW's EESM (wound-rotor, magnet-free) is fully commercial, and Tesla showed a rare-earth-free Cybercab drive unit in September 2026, though its materials are undisclosed. Niron's iron-nitride magnet, the only new US magnet chemistry nearing scale, starts production at Sartell in 2027 (1,500 t/yr). Its energy product is below NdFeB, so it substitutes in lower-performance uses rather than in traction motors at first.

### Cited Findings
**Niron Magnetics (Minnesota)**
- Construction of the Sartell, MN plant began in September 2025. It is designed for 1,500 t/yr of iron-nitride magnets, with production from 2027 and about 175 jobs. — [Enterprise Minnesota, Summer 2026](https://www.enterpriseminnesota.org/articles/summer-2026/positive-attraction/); [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/niron-magnetics-breaks-ground-on-worlds-first-commercial-iron-nitride-magnet-plant-a-rare-earth-free-milestone-with-strategic-consequences/)

This adds to a $150M conditional commitment from the DoW Office of Strategic Capital and $17.5M from ARPA-E SCALEUP. — [MPR News, Aug 18 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/18/niron-magnetics-150-million-tribal-loan-advances-plan); [Niron](https://nironmagnetics.com/niron-magnetics-receives-150-million-loan-from-the-shakopee-mdewakanton-sioux-community-to-support-iron-nitride-magnet-manufacturing/)

**Tesla**
- About September 3, 2026, at a Cybercab event, Tesla showed a new drive unit that Musk confirmed is rare-earth-free. - A peer-reviewed overview of rare-earth-free EV motors (induction, EESM, switched reluctance, ferrite-assisted synchronous reluctance) covers the trade-offs in power density and efficiency. — [MDPI Machines 13(8):702, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/13/8/702)

### Inferences
- Researcher's TRL estimates: EESM TRL 9, in mass production at BMW and Renault (Zoe/Megane). Induction motors TRL 9 (Tesla front/legacy). Ferrite-assisted synchronous reluctance TRL 8–9. Tesla's RE-free unit TRL 8, in low-volume deployment with materials undisclosed. Niron FeN TRL 7, moving to 8 at Sartell in 2027. Grain-boundary diffusion (GBD), Dy-lean NdFeB TRL 9, the industry standard in Japan and China. Ce/La-substituted NdFeB TRL 8–9 for lower grades. SmCo TRL 9 for defense and high-temperature niches.
- Dy/Tb is where substitution and reduction pay off fastest. GBD cuts heavy REE use substantially, and HRE-free traction magnets already exist in Japan. China also leads in GBD at scale. Because Dy/Tb, not Nd/Pr, is the most acute choke point (China's April 2025 controls targeted medium and heavy REEs), adoption of HRE-free designs by US OEMs is a 1–3 year lever.
- Tesla's announcement should be treated as partially hype until the material and production scale are disclosed. Even if fleet-wide, it would cut about 1 kg of REE per car. That matters for supply risk but does not remove US magnet demand from wind, defense, robotics, HDDs and industrial motors.
- Niron's 1,500 t/yr is about 5–10% of US NdFeB consumption in volume terms, but FeN's lower energy product means more magnet mass per application. Its early markets are speakers, sensors, small motors and HVAC, not traction drives.

### Gaps
- Toyota's 2018 Nd-reduced (Ce/La) magnet and Daido/Honda's 2016 HRE-free hot-deformed traction magnet: not re-verified with sources this session.
- Niron's verified magnetic properties ((BH)max) and cost per kg vs ferrite/NdFeB: no independent data found.
- No data on US SmCo capacity (e.g., Arnold Magnetic, Electron Energy) in 2026.

## 4. China's technology lead, export bans on technology and equipment, the patent landscape, and the know-how gap

### Takeaway
On December 21, 2023, China banned export of REE extraction and separation technology and some magnet-making technology. In October 2025 (MOFCOM Announcements 61/62) it extended controls to mining, smelting and separation, metals, magnet manufacturing, recycling technologies and services, plus extraterritorial rules. Those October 2025 measures were later postponed until about November 2026. China dominates patent volume (about 81% of REE patent families filed 2014–2024), while Japan and the US retain strength in high-value downstream patents. The real gap is operational know-how and scale, and published estimates of "years behind" are commentary rather than measurement.

### Cited Findings
- December 21, 2023: MOFCOM added REE extraction and separation technology, REE metal and alloy production technology, and some rare earth magnet preparation technology to the Catalogue of Technologies Prohibited and Restricted from Export. — [Inquirer/Reuters](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=1878126); [Andersen Institute](https://anderseninstitute.org/chinas-export-control-architecture-and-its-use-of-critical-minerals-as-strategic-pressure-points/)
- October 9, 2025: MOFCOM Announcement 62 controls technologies for REE mining, smelting and separation, metal production, magnet manufacturing, and recycling of secondary REE resources. It requires licenses where non-controlled goods or services materially contribute to such activities abroad. Announcement 61 added extraterritorial rules (licenses for foreign-made magnets with trace Chinese-origin REE). — [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202510/1345192.shtml); [Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/china-expands-export-controls-on-rare-earths-magnets-and-high-tech-materials-what-companies-need-to-know/); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-new-rare-earth-and-magnet-restrictions-threaten-us-defense-supply-chains)
- Status conflict: CIRS says the October 2025 measures were suspended after the November 2025 US–China arrangement. CFR says they are "now postponed until November 2026". The December 2023 technology-catalogue ban was not reported as suspended. — [CIRS](https://www.cirs-group.com/en/chemicals/china-temporarily-suspends-export-controls-on-key-raw-materials-including-rare-earths-lithium-batteries-and-diamond); [CFR](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon)
- Patents: a 2026 landscape report found China accounted for 81% of rare-earth-related patent families filed 2014–2024. — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/chinas-rare-earth-patent-weakness-investors-should-read-beyond-the-headlines/)
- LexisNexis IP: China leads in absolute patent counts but lags its two major competitors on quality (Patent Asset Index). — [LexisNexis IP](https://www.lexisnexisip.com/resources/china-leads-in-rare-earth-elements-yet-portfolio-strength-lags/)
- A University of Science and Technology of China study (reported in SCMP, 2026) found that Japan and the US control key patents for advanced rare earth functional materials. Japan leads overall in permanent magnets, the US leads in catalytic, luminescent and polishing materials, and China leads only in some alloy technologies. Critics note the study's methodology is unclear. — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/science/article/3359466/chinas-rare-earth-industry-has-critical-weakness-researchers-warn); [Rare Earth Exchanges critique](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/chinas-rare-earth-patent-weakness-investors-should-read-beyond-the-headlines/)
- The know-how gap is a "strategic technological challenge, not just a pollution problem". — [New Security Beat, Aug 2024](https://www.newsecuritybeat.org/2024/08/mine-the-tech-gap-why-chinas-rare-earth-dominance-persists/)
- "Clearly, China is the leader, and the U.S. is far behind" (a mining executive, quoted in Fortune, March 11, 2026). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/11/china-us-rare-earth-processing-critical-minerals)
- Scale benchmark: China Northern Rare Earth reportedly began trial production on an eight-furnace expansion of about 50,000 t/yr of NdFeB strip-cast alloy. — [Rare Earth Exchanges tracker](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/ex-china-rare-earth-supply-chain-signal-tracker/)

### Inferences
- The technology ban mainly blocks transfer of Chinese process packages, engineers and plant design: the tacit SX cascade know-how for separating 15+ elements at scale. It does not block US-originated IP. This raises the value of US-owned alternatives (RapidSX, chromatography, Phoenix) even if their unit costs are higher.
- "Years behind": no rigorous source quantifies it. Industry commentary commonly says 10–20 years behind in scale and operational experience for separation and magnet manufacturing. This is commentary only and should be flagged as such.
- The patent-quality finding suggests the US/Japan gap is in industrialization, not invention.

### Gaps
- No WIPO or CSIS primary patent study retrieved. The 81% figure comes from a secondary summary of a 2026 landscape report whose originator was not identified.
- The exact current legal status of the October 2025 technology and equipment controls (suspended vs postponed to November 2026) is unresolved. Check MOFCOM directly.

## 5. Equipment supply chain (strip casters, hydrogen decrepitation, jet mills, sintering furnaces): sources outside China

### Takeaway
Little reliable public information was found. Japan (e.g., ULVAC for vacuum induction melting and continuous vacuum sintering furnaces) is the main proven non-China source of magnet-line equipment. China's controls are reported to restrict exports of specialized sintering and milling equipment. Equipment availability and lead times are flagged by the IEA as a weakness of emerging supply chains.

### Cited Findings
- A sintered NdFeB line needs induction melting furnaces, strip casters, hydrogen decrepitation units, jet mills, isostatic presses, sintering furnaces and precision machining. This makes it far more capital-intensive than bonded magnet lines. — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/ndfeb-magnet-manufacturing-plant-project-report)
- May 2026: EMAT signed eight contracts with ULVAC Korea for 13 machines, including vacuum induction melting and continuous vacuum sintering furnaces. — [Rare Earth Exchanges investor briefing](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/rare-earth-magnet-production-outside-china-recently-investor-briefing/)
- China's curbs mean "cutting-edge equipment, such as specialized sintering ovens and milling machines for magnet alloys, cannot be easily acquired by new overseas competitors". This analysis is about 13 months old. — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/rare-earth-magnet-production-outside-china-recently-investor-briefing/)
- The IEA identifies metallisation, alloy production, specialised equipment and skilled labour as key weaknesses in emerging supply chains. — [IEA report PDF](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)

### Inferences
- Non-China equipment exists, mainly from Japanese vacuum-furnace and milling OEMs and from European furnace makers, but lead times and limited capacity make equipment a pacing item for US magnet plants. Chinese-made equipment, previously the low-cost default, is now subject to licensing risk.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session: Germany's ALD Vacuum Technologies (VIM/strip casting), Hosokawa Alpine and NETZSCH (jet mills), and Japan's Shimadzu/Chugai Ro (sintering). Also not verified: whether US plants (MP Fort Worth, Vulcan, USA Rare Earth Stillwater, Noveon, e-VAC Sumter) rely on Chinese equipment, and typical lead times.
- No primary source found for the scope of China's October 2025 controls on magnet-making equipment specifically.

## 6. US national lab and university programs and funding (CMI, DOE, ARPA-E, DARPA)

### Takeaway
DOE's Critical Materials Innovation Hub (CMI, led by Ames Lab) is in its third five-year phase (from November 2023) at up to about $30M per year. Program-level funding is small compared with the multi-hundred-million-dollar DoD/DOE loans now going to companies (Vulcan/ReElement about $700M, Phoenix $566M, Niron $150M). The US approach shifted in 2025–26 from R&D toward capital deployment and price support.

### Cited Findings
- CMI was renewed for a third five-year phase beginning November 2023, with potential funding of $30M per year. — [Windfair/DOE](https://w3.windfair.net/wind-energy/news/45188-department-of-energy-usa-doe-critical-material-raw-material-rare-earth-r-d-innovation-hib-clean-energy-green-net-zero-industry)
- A November 2024 CMI funding call offered up to $10M for early-stage R&D on REEs, gallium and copper (up to eight awards, max $1.5M each). — [Ames Lab](https://www.ameslab.gov/news/us-doe-announces-10m-funding-for-critical-materials-innovation-hub); [DOE AMMTO](https://www.energy.gov/eere/ammto/articles/doe-energy-innovation-hub-announces-10-million-early-stage-research-development)
- In 2026, Ames Lab won a $1.5M DOE award ("MICRO-vision") for rare-earth ore processing research. — [Yahoo/Ames Lab](https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/ames-lab-snags-1-5m-110608323.html)
- September 30, 2026: the US invested $29.5M to develop critical minerals technology. — [Forbes, Sept 30 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/noelfletcher/2026/09/30/us-invests-295-million-to-develop-critical-minerals-technology/)
- ARPA-E SCALEUP gave $17.5M to Niron. — [Niron/Enterprise Minnesota](https://www.enterpriseminnesota.org/articles/summer-2026/positive-attraction/)
- Phoenix Tailings has a $66M DOE grant and a conditional $500M loan commitment. — [RBJ](https://rbj.net/2026/10/21/phoenix-tailings-rochester-375m-rare-earth-facility/)

### Inferences
- The R&D pipeline (CMI, ORNL, LLNL lanmodulin, ARPA-E) feeds technologies at TRL 3–6 that will not reach commercial scale before about 2030. Technologies that can shorten the 2026–2030 timeline are those already at TRL 7+: EESM/induction/ferrite motors, GBD Dy-lean magnets, mechanical and M2M recycling, RapidSX and chromatography, and Niron FeN for non-traction uses.

### Gaps
- Details of the September 30, 2026 $29.5M award (recipients, program) were not retrieved.
- DARPA programs (e.g., EMBER, an REE bio-mining/biotechnology program) and ARPA-E rare-earth programs (e.g., REEACH, magnet substitution) were not verified this session.
- No consolidated figure found for total annual federal REE R&D spend.
