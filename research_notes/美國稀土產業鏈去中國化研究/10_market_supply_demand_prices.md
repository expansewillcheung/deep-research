# Global Rare Earth Market Fundamentals: Supply, Demand, Prices (as of 22 Oct 2026)

Research note for the investor-report writer. All prices are dated. "China domestic" means SMM, CREIA or Asian Metal-style yuan quotes, which usually include 13% VAT. "Ex-China" means Rotterdam/Europe, CIF North America or bilateral contract prices. Ex-China assessments rest on thin, over-the-counter trade, so figures from different sources often disagree; the conflicts are flagged below.

Research limit: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Some items (Benchmark and Project Blue balance numbers, a 2026 China quota figure, China state-reserve buying, the JOGMEC stockpile size, a full 2025 monthly China NdPr series) could not be checked and are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Global REO production by country, 2023–2025 (USGS), 2026 outlook, and China quotas

### Takeaway
USGS (MCS 2026, Feb 2026) puts 2025 world mine output at about 390,000 t REO. China is flat at 270,000 t (~69%), the US is up to 51,000 t (#2), Australia is at 29,000 t and Myanmar (Burma) fell to 22,000 t. Beijing stopped publishing quotas after 2024. The visible "supply" story is now refining and export licensing rather than mining.

### Inferences
- The mined-supply data are flat in China (270 kt three years running on USGS figures) and growing ex-China (US +12% in 2025). Effective supply to non-Chinese buyers, however, depends on Chinese export licensing for separated heavies, not on mine tonnage. For 2026, a reasonable working assumption is world mine output of roughly 390–420 kt REO, with China ~270 kt+ (quota unpublished) and growth from the US, Australia (Lynas ramp) and Brazil. This is an inference only; no USGS or industry 2026 estimate was found.
- The 10 Nov 2026 expiry of China's suspension is the nearest binary supply catalyst for the report's stress tests.

### Gaps
- No official 2025 or 2026 China quota figures were found from MIIT or Xinhua. The 2025 270k/255k figure is unverified.
- No 2026 production estimates were found by country.
- 2023 figures come from MCS 2025 and may since have been revised. MCS 2026 shows only 2024–2025.

## 2. Demand by element and application, growth drivers, and forecasts and deficits to 2030/2035

### Takeaway
Magnets account for more than half of REE demand by volume and nearly all of its value. IEA (STEPS) sees magnet REE (Nd, Pr, Dy, Tb) demand up ~25% from 2025 to 2030, to ~120 kt REE, and calls the global market "well supplied" to 2040. The squeeze it sees is ex-China refining and magnet capacity. Sell-side and consultancy forecasts (Canaccord, Macquarie, BMI, WoodMac, Adamas) mostly show an NdPr deficit in 2026–2028, roughly balanced around 2029–2031, and renewed deficits by 2035. The forecasts differ widely by vintage.

### Cited Findings
**IEA GCMO 2026** ([PDF](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf))
- Magnet REE demand has doubled since 2015. In STEPS it grows by about a quarter between 2025 and 2030, approaching 120 kt REE, and by more than 80% to 2050 (170 kt REE).
- Drivers are EVs, wind, industrial motors and automation. "Automation, robotics and digital technologies" play a larger role beyond 2030.
- Magnet uses are more than half of total REE demand.
- Non-magnet heavies grow more slowly: Gd and Ho at ~4%/yr; Y at 2.5%/yr to 2030.
- Ex-China magnet REE demand grows ~50% over the next decade. Asia ex-China accounts for about half of ex-China consumption.
- Supply: mined magnet REE supply rises 30% to 104 kt REE in 2035 (base case), plus 21 kt in the high case. Refined supply reaches 125 kt in the high case. Ex-China mining and refining triple to 57 kt and 38 kt REE respectively by 2035 (high case).
- Downstream gap: announced ex-China metal, alloy and magnet projects total only ~57 kt NdFeB in 2035, about 18 kt of magnet REE content. Current and planned ex-China capacity covers ~50% of ex-China demand for mining, 25% for refining and "well below 20%" for magnets in 2035.
- Balance: "Projected supply remains broadly sufficient through to 2040" globally. The binding risk is concentration and export controls.
- Investment: just over $60 billion is needed in rare earths over the next decade.
- Value chain: REEs are 40% of NdFeB magnet value. Dy is ~20% of magnet price, NdPr almost 15% and Tb ~10%. REEs are ~5% of an EV motor's price and below 1% of an EV's. Tripling REE prices would almost double magnet prices and raise motor prices by ~10%.

### Inferences
- There are two balances. The global balance, including China, is near equilibrium; the IEA calls it well supplied, and Canaccord sees ~7% short in 2026. The ex-China balance is badly short in midstream and magnets: refining covers ~25% and magnets <20% of ex-China demand by 2035 even with announced projects (IEA). Investment theses depending on "NdPr deficit means higher global price" are weaker than theses depending on "ex-China qualified supply is scarce and will earn a premium or a floor".
- Heavies (Dy and Tb) are the tighter element market (Canaccord's 12% deficit; IEA's ~5x premium). Thrifting through grain-boundary diffusion, and Ho or Gd substitution, is the main demand-side risk to Dy/Tb theses.
- Older Adamas deficit numbers (2021–2023) are widely recycled in 2026 secondary media. The report should label them as dated.

### Gaps
- Benchmark Mineral Intelligence and Project Blue numeric NdPr balances for 2030/2035 could not be retrieved (behind paywall, or the search budget ran out).
- No current 2025/2026 split of demand by application (EV/wind/robotics/defense/AI data centres) with percentages was found from a primary source.
- No quantified humanoid-robot or AI-data-centre NdPr demand forecast was retrieved.

## 3. Prices: history, current levels, and China domestic vs ex-China

### Takeaway
NdPr oxide went from a 2022 peak (~$175/kg per Canaccord; USGS 2022 average $124/kg) to a 2024 trough ($55/kg USGS average; ~$50/kg low). It nearly doubled from October 2025 to ~$125/kg in February 2026 (IEA) and peaked around April 2026. Since mid-2026 it has settled at about CNY 720–750k/t in China, roughly $97–110/kg depending on VAT and FX. Ex-China NdPr is near the $110/kg floor: it was below it in August–September at $95/kg CIF, and Canaccord says it was above $110 at Rotterdam in October. Heavies and yttrium carry large ex-China premiums: Dy ~4–5x at Rotterdam (up to ~15x for CIF North America quotes), Tb ~4–7x and yttrium ~80–130x.

## 4. Inventory/stockpiling behavior and market structure/size

### Takeaway
Government stockpiling is now a major price-setting force outside China: the US Project Vault ($12B), the US NDS, G7 coordinated stockpiling, and Japan (JOGMEC/JARE) with its stockpiles being drawn down. The rare earth market is small, a single-digit billions of dollars a year, and opaque. China dominates both price discovery and supply.

### Cited Findings
- **US Project Vault**, launched 2 Feb 2026: a ~$12B Strategic Critical Minerals Reserve, funded by a $10B EXIM loan plus ~$1.67–2B of private capital (GM, Stellantis, GE Vernova, Google, Boeing). Hartree, Mercuria and Traxys procure the material. It initially sources "from anywhere in the world, including China", then moves to a domestic/allied-first replenishment model. — [Fortune, 3 Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/03/project-vault-critical-minerals-stockpile-rare-earths-first-step-break-china-chokehold); [MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/web/us-critical-mineral-inventory-plan-includes-buying-china-metals/); [Holland & Hart](https://www.hollandhart.com/breaking-chinas-stranglehold-trumps-12b-critical-minerals-stockpile)
- **US National Defense Stockpile:** FY2025 potential acquisitions were 300 t NdPr oxide, 450 t NdFeB magnet block, 60 t SmCo alloy and 1,100 t lanthanum. The FY2026 Annual Materials Plan was not released. — [USGS MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths.pdf)
- **G7:** coordinated critical-mineral stockpiling agreed on 17 Jun 2026, with lithium and nickel as initial pilots. — [Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/06/japan-seeks-g7-price-floors-to-break-chinas-rare-earth-grip/)
- **Japan:** JARE (JOGMEC + Sojitz) holds the Lynas floor-price offtake. — [Lynas Q3 FY26](https://wcsecure.weblink.com.au/pdf/LYC/03080721.pdf). A Japanese official said in Oct 2026 that yttrium "Stockpiles are decreasing and rare earth saving efforts have a limit." — [Yahoo/Newsmax, 20 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/japan-nears-rare-earth-shortage-135934672.html)
- **MP inventory:** ~650 t of NdPr oxide and metal on hand or in transit at 30 Jun 2026. — [MP Q2 2026 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/13/mp-materials-mp-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- **Market size:**
  - IEA: the market size for many strategic minor minerals "is often below USD 10 billion. Like rare earths…" It describes rare earths as "small, opaque and volatile… dominant price-setting producers and limited price transparency". — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)

- Order-of-magnitude market size: ~390 kt REO at an average value of a few $/kg to tens of $/kg puts the total market at roughly $5–10B a year. Small absolute volumes mean a single buyer (Project Vault, NDS, JARE) can move the ex-China price.

## 5. Key sensitivities: a Western price floor vs a Chinese supply flood

### Takeaway
At today's prices, NdPr floors at $110/kg add a modest ~$10–15/kg to Western producers' realized prices. This matters for EBITDA but does not drive the equity value. The bigger value at risk is in heavies and yttrium: premiums of 4–130x exist only because of Chinese export licensing. A Chinese relaxation or flood would hit Dy/Tb/Y ex-China revenue far harder than NdPr revenue. A return of NdPr to the 2024 trough (~$50–55/kg) would put unfloored ex-China producers deep underwater, and floored producers would shift the loss to governments.

### Cited Findings
- **IEA modelling:**
  - A price floor set at US$112,000/t NdPr oxide was modelled for an average diversified refining project.
  - Cap-and-floor mechanisms raise the project internal rate of return (IRR) by **+2.4 percentage points**, at a gross fiscal cost of ~US$18M/yr per project over the first 15 years.
  - A cap-and-floor contract for difference with floors of $85,000/t or $65,000/t and a cap of $110,000/t, covering half of the remaining ex-China demand to 2035, costs ~US$3.7B gross to 2035 (~US$370M/yr) at an $85k floor.
  - CAPEX and OPEX support is more fiscally efficient than price support at lowering levelised cost.
  — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- IEA: "The elevated prices are likely to be temporary". It saw a marginal downward correction after the Feb 2026 peak. — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- Benchmark (Aug 2026): ex-China NdPr oversupply persists until US and EU magnet demand strengthens or producers cut output. The floor "guarantees… a minimum return regardless of spot prices". — [Benchmark](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/ex-china-ndpr-prices-slip-below-110-kg-price-floor-as-oversupply-builds). The floor is "doing its job as insurance and failing as a signal". — [Hard Energy Fund](https://thehardenergyfund.substack.com/p/1075-inside-china-7500-outside-same)
- Canaccord's price deck has ex-China yttrium falling from $740/kg to $100/kg by 2030, so the market already expects premium compression in yttrium. — [ZeroHedge repost](https://ifttt.itbehere.com/2026/10/20/the-great-rare-earth-disconnect-prices-up-23-miners-down-28/)
- Demand-side cap: a tripling of REE prices raises EV motor prices by only ~10% and EV prices by under 1%, so OEMs can absorb high prices. Magnet makers' margins, by contrast, are badly squeezed (magnet price almost doubles). — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)

### Inferences (illustrative arithmetic; not sourced forecasts)
- **NdPr price sensitivity.** Each US$10/kg change in NdPr is worth about:
  - MP: ~US$40M/yr in revenue, on an annualised ~4,000 t NdPr (Q3 2026 guidance is >1,000 t/quarter).
  - Lynas: ~US$75M/yr, on ~7,500 t/yr (Q4 FY26 run-rate of 1,857 t/quarter).
  For context, MP's Q2 2026 Materials Adj. EBITDA was $32.5M, of which PPA contributed $17.6M. Without the PPA, Materials EBITDA would have been about $15M, so the floor currently supplies roughly half of segment EBITDA.
- **Floor-vs-spot gap.** With ex-China spot at $95–97/kg (Aug–Sep 2026), a $110 floor is worth ~$13–15/kg. That is ~US$50–60M/yr for MP at 4 kt, and up to ~US$65–75M/yr for Lynas on the 5,000 t JARE tranche, if fully drawn. If spot rises above $110 (Rotterdam in October per Canaccord), the floor is worth zero and Lynas shares upside to JARE above $150 (capped at $10M/yr).
- **Chinese flood or relaxation scenario.**
  - NdPr back to the 2024 average of $55/kg (USGS) is ~$40–55/kg below today. Unfloored ex-China separators (developers without offtake floors) would be cash-negative, if typical ex-China costs are in the $60–90/kg range. This cost range is an assumption not verified in this session. Floored producers (MP, Lynas on its JARE and US tranches) would be protected on contracted volumes, and the cost moves to the US DoD and Japan's JARE.
  - In Dy/Tb, compression of premiums from ~4.7x to ~1.5x (Canaccord Rotterdam vs China) would cut ex-China Tb from ~$4,600 to ~$1,500/kg and Dy from ~$1,070 to ~$340/kg. USA Rare Earth's contract floors ($575 Dy, $2,050 Tb) would then bind.
  - Yttrium has the most downside: Canaccord itself models $740 to $100/kg by 2030.
- **Key upcoming catalysts** for stress tests:
  1. 10 Nov 2026: expiry of China's one-year suspension of the October 2025 controls.
  2. 1 Jan 2027: the DFARS magnet ban takes effect.
  3. Whether the G7 turns the floor-price proposal into policy.
  4. The pace of Project Vault purchases.
  5. Ex-China magnet capacity ramp (MP Independence/10X, GM deliveries in Q4 2026). This determines whether the ex-China NdPr "oversupply" Benchmark flagged clears.

- No quantitative scenario from a named consultancy (Benchmark, Project Blue, WoodMac, Adamas) on a Chinese supply flood was found.
