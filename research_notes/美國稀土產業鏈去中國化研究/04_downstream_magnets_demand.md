# US Downstream Rare Earth Permanent Magnets (NdFeB, SmCo) and US End-Use Demand — as of Oct 2026

Research note compiled 2026-10-22. The session's web-search budget ran out before a few follow-ups (DFARS waiver status in Oct 2026, GM motor strategy, Arnold Magnetic, Quadrant, Lockheed/RTX sourcing), so those items appear under Gaps. Figures marked "announced" are company targets. Figures marked "operating" have documented production.

## 1. US magnet makers: capacity (operating vs planned), start dates, customers, 2026 progress

### Takeaway
As of October 2026, US sintered NdFeB nameplate capacity that has actually started production is about 5,000–5,600 tpa: MP Independence 1,000, eVAC Sumter 2,000, Noveon ~2,000 and USA Rare Earth (USAR) Stillwater ramping toward 600. Real output is far lower. The US made roughly 300 t in 2025, and in 2026 MP and USAR were still in customer qualification with little or no commercial magnet revenue. Announced capacity for about 2028–2030 is roughly 35,000+ tpa, led by MP 10X (7,000), USAR Blacksburg (6,400), Vulcan Benson (10,000) and the eVAC expansion. Nearly all of it is scheduled to commission in 2028 or later.

### Inferences
- Plants that have started production (MP, eVAC, Noveon, USAR) add up to about **5,000–5,600 tpa of NdFeB nameplate capacity**. 2026 output is probably well below that, perhaps 1,000–2,500 t at most (my estimate). The reasons: MP and USAR were still in qualification with little or no commercial magnet revenue through Q2 2026, and eVAC itself guided 1,500–1,600 t.
- Announced US capacity reaches **about 35,000–40,000 tpa** by about 2029–30:
  - MP 10,000
  - USAR 10,000
  - Vulcan 10,000
  - eVAC 2,000 (path to 12,000)
  - Noveon about 2,000+
  - Evolution 6,400–10,000 (unproven)
  - Iron nitride, separately: Niron 1,500 plus 10,000.
- About 70% of that capacity commissions in 2028 or later, and qualification usually adds 12–24 months. Meaningful volume beyond about 10,000 tpa is therefore unlikely before 2029–2030.
- Consolidation is under way. Energy Fuels is buying VAC, and USAR has bought LCM, Serra Verde and TMRC. US magnet makers are integrating vertically into heavy rare earths, metals and alloys, which Adamas names as the near-term bottlenecks.

### Gaps

## 2. US NdFeB demand, import share from China, and the 2025 export-control shock

### Takeaway
US direct (customs-visible) NdFeB magnet imports were about 9,400 t in 2024 and about 8,300 t in 2025 under HS 8505.11. China's share by weight rose from 75% to 85% because non-Chinese supply fell faster. Including magnets embedded in imported finished goods, US NdFeB consumption is about 40,000–48,000 t/yr. China's April 2025 licensing regime cut magnet exports to the US by 81% month-on-month in May 2025 (to 46 t). They rebounded in June, but shipments in H1 2026 were still about 20% below the 2022–24 average. US domestic output (about 300 t in 2025) covered roughly 3% of direct imports and under 1% of total embedded demand.

The narrower line HTS 8505.11.0070 covers sintered NdFeB specifically. — Search summary of a Columbia-linked trade-data analysis; the China share is corroborated by [REEx, Sep 13, 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/us-ndfeb-magnet-supply-chain-china/). Exact tonnages are not verified against USITC DataWeb.
- In 2023 China supplied 93.3% of US NdFeB magnet imports (by value, per the USITC). — [USITC JICE paper](https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/journals/jice_recovering_rare_earth_elements_from_e_waste.pdf)

### Inferences
- Direct US imports fell by about 1,100 t in 2025 while Chinese tonnage held flat. The drop came from non-Chinese sources (Japan, Vietnam, Philippines and others), possibly because those producers themselves depend on Chinese heavy rare earth feedstock. Japan's imports of Chinese heavy rare earths fell about 80% in H1 2026 (see section 6). This is my inference; the country breakdown was not retrieved.
- In 2025 the US depended on China for about 85% of directly imported NdFeB, and higher still once embedded imports are counted (China makes about 94% of global sintered magnets). Domestic production was under 1% of total consumption.
- If commercial supply comes through, 2026 US output of perhaps 1,000–2,500 t would cover about 10–25% of direct import volume, but only 2–5% of total embedded consumption (inference).

### Gaps
- Monthly 2025–2026 US Census/USITC import data for HTS 8505.11.0070 were not retrieved, and the 2024/2025 tonnages above have not been checked against DataWeb.
- No value data, unit values or country breakdown beyond China.
- No official US demand figure for 2026. The 40–48 kt figures are analyst estimates.

## 3. Demand by sector and growth to 2030/2035

### Takeaway
No authoritative public breakdown of US magnet demand by sector exists. Commerce/BIS (Section 232, 2021) expected EVs and wind to make up 55% of US NdFeB demand by 2030. Adamas forecasts US direct demand growing about 17%/yr to about 50 kt by 2035, roughly five times today's level. Defense is strategically critical but small in volume: Adamas says US defense uses only "tens-to-hundreds of tonnes" of magnet rare earths per year, under 0.1% of global rare earth demand. Robotics is the main new upside, approaching about 1,000 t/yr by 2030 per Adamas.

### Cited Findings
- **BIS Section 232 NdFeB report:** US demand forecast to more than double from 2020 to 2030. EVs and wind are forecast to reach 55% of US demand by 2030 and 61% by 2050, and the totals include embedded magnets. A 2020 baseline of "about 16,100 t" appeared in a search summary but was not verified in the PDF. — [BIS Section 232 appendices](https://media.bis.gov/media/documents/section-232-ndfeb-magnets-report-appendices-combined.pdf)
- **Adamas forecasts:**
  - US NdFeB demand grows about 17%/yr, the fastest of any major market.
  - US direct demand reaches about 50 kt by 2035 (excluding imported assemblies).
  - Global demand roughly 500+ kt by 2035, versus about 607 kt by Reuters.
  - Source: [REEx, Sep 1, 2025](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/will-america-be-rare-earth-magnet-independent-anytime-soon-no-rare-earth-magnet-demand-vs-supply-u-s-and-china-through-2030-and-beyond/)
  - REEx's own extrapolation: 20–25 kt direct and more than 50 kt including embedded by 2030. — same source
  - Caveat from the same source: US cuts to green-energy incentives (the OBBBA rollback of EV and wind credits) could lower this trajectory.
- **EVs:** 1–2 kg of NdFeB per EV; a large wind turbine needs hundreds of kg. — [REEx, Sep 1, 2025](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/will-america-be-rare-earth-magnet-independent-anytime-soon-no-rare-earth-magnet-demand-vs-supply-u-s-and-china-through-2030-and-beyond/)
  - About 95% of new EV motors still used rare earth magnets in 2025. — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/case-studies/ev-motors-rare-earth-magnets)
- **Defense:**
  - GAO (September 2024): DOD's total rare earth demand is under 0.1% of global demand.
  - Adamas (Ryan Castilloux): US defense uses "tens-to-hundreds of tonnes of magnet rare earths per annum." Adamas expects defense to be among the fastest-growing end uses through 2040, driven by robotics, advanced air mobility and autonomous systems.
  - Source: [Adamas, May 23, 2025](https://www.adamasintel.com/rees-in-defense-commercially-insignificant-strategically-imperative/)
  - F-35: about 417 kg of rare earth materials across 47 subsystems. This is the widely cited figure; Adamas says actual elemental content is lower. Virginia-class submarine: more than 4,100 kg. About 78% of Pentagon programs (F-35, Virginia-class, Tomahawk and others) fall under DFARS magnet restrictions (Adamas/FT). — [TechTimes, Oct 2, 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
  - Lower-reliability claim from an unnamed industry report: the Pentagon needs 3,000–4,000 t of specialized magnets per year, rising to about 10,000 t by 2030. It conflicts with the Adamas estimate and should be treated as unverified. — (search summary; no primary source identified)
- **Humanoid robots / Tesla Optimus:**
  - About 3.5 kg of NdFeB per humanoid across more than 40 servo actuators.
  - Adamas: 36,700 units in 2027 would need only 100–150 t; demand approaches about 1,000 tpa by 2030.
  - Adamas production view: Tesla output about 4,000 in 2026 and 18,000 in 2027, and Tesla could take half the US humanoid market by 2027.
  - Musk's target: about 1M units/yr by 2030.
  - Tesla's Optimus program was disrupted by Chinese magnet controls in April 2025.
  - Source: [Adamas](https://www.adamasintel.com/de/tesla-could-capture-half-the-humanoid-robot-market-in-the-us-by-2027/); [IIoT World](https://iiot-world.com/smart-manufacturing/tesla-optimus-manufacturing-2026/); [The Register, Apr 23, 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/23/tesla_optimus_china/)
- **Adamas ex-China capacity pipeline:** operating or planned ex-China NdFeB capacity for 2030 rose 50% in Q3 2025 and another 11% in Q4. It reached nearly 80,000 tpa by January 2026 and was expected to approach or exceed 100,000 tpa during 2026. — [Adamas Top Predictions for 2026](https://www.adamasintel.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Adamas-Intelligence-Rare-Earth-Magnet-Market-Outlook-Top-Predictions-for-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rough sector shape of US direct demand (my synthesis, not a sourced breakdown):
  - EV and hybrid traction plus wind are the largest blocks (about 50%+ by 2030 per BIS, though now weakened by US policy changes).
  - Industrial motors, HVAC and consumer electronics make up most of the rest, and much of that arrives embedded.
  - Data-center HDDs and cooling: no figure found.
  - Defense is about 1–5% by tonnage but carries high Dy/Tb and SmCo intensity.
  - Humanoid robots: under 1 kt by 2030.
- Because defense volume is small (hundreds to low thousands of tonnes of finished magnets), **defense NdFeB needs could in principle be covered by 2027–28** from MP Independence, eVAC and USAR Stillwater once these are qualified. The real constraint is Dy/Tb and samarium supply and DFARS-compliant upstream feed, not magnet-forming capacity.
- **Commercial direct demand** (about 10–25 kt by 2030) would need roughly the full announced build-out (10X, Blacksburg, Vulcan) to be running by 2029–2031. Embedded demand (more than 30 kt) will not be displaced by US magnet plants alone.

### Gaps
- No sourced US tonnage by sector (EV, wind, electronics, industrial, HDD/data center, drones). The Adamas/Benchmark regional breakdowns are paywalled.
- Drone magnet demand (including DoD drone-dominance programs) was not quantified.
- No sourced F-35-to-magnet-tonnage conversion, and no figures for missile programs.

## 4. Automaker and OEM strategies, rare earth-free designs, stockpiling

### Takeaway
US OEMs are pursuing three things at once: (1) anchor offtakes with US magnet makers (GM with MP, VAC and Noveon; Apple with MP; DoD with MP's 10X), (2) rare earth-free or heavy rare earth-reduced motor development, and (3) inventory buffers. The buffers proved thin in 2025, when Ford, Suzuki and others halted lines within weeks. Magnet-free traction motors remain years from scale; about 95% of new EV motors used rare earth magnets in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **GM:**
  - MP delivered qualification magnets to GM, with commercial shipments expected in Q4 2026. — [MarketBeat, Aug 8, 2026](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/mp-materials-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-08/)
  - MP sells magnetic precursor products to GM; revenue began in Q1 2025. — [MP 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001801368/000180136826000048/mp-20260630.htm)
  - GM has a multi-year supply agreement with Noveon. — [Noveon](https://noveon.co/noveon-completes-series-c)
  - Stellantis, GM and Honda have each announced funding or partnerships to develop alternatives to rare earth permanent magnets. — [Rest of World, 2026](https://restofworld.org/2026/chinas-rare-earth-dominance-is-hard-for-ev-makers-to-escape/)
- **Apple:** $500M commitment for US recycled magnets from MP Independence, with shipments from 2027. — [MP](https://mpmaterials.com/news/mp-materials-and-apple-announce-500-million-partnership-to-produce-recycled-rare-earth-magnets-in-the-united-states)
- **Tesla:** said at its 2023 Investor Day that its next-generation motor would use no rare earth elements. — [Electrek (via search)](https://electrek.co/2026/07/13/vimag-labs-magnet-free-ev-motor-patent/)
- **Ford:** production pauses in 2025 (see section 2). I found no Ford statement on magnet-free motors or stockpiles. — [Ford Authority](https://fordauthority.com/2025/07/ford-plants-shut-down-for-weeks-over-rare-earth-magnet-shortage)
- **Stockpiling:** a magnet supplier executive for Hyundai said inventories built earlier "have already been depleted." A Toyota purchasing executive said "They can shut us down in two months, the entire auto industry." — [Rest of World, 2026](https://restofworld.org/2026/chinas-rare-earth-dominance-is-hard-for-ev-makers-to-escape/)
- **Magnet-free timelines:** Valeo's magnet-free motor is at least three years from production; Renault and Valeo target about 2027–28. — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/case-studies/ev-motors-rare-earth-magnets); [REEx](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/ev-motor-rare-earth-substitution/)
- **Iron-nitride route:** Niron's rare earth-free 1,500 tpa plant (early 2027) offers OEMs a non-rare-earth option for lower-performance applications. — [Niron](https://nironmagnetics.com/niron-magnetics-advances-u-s-permanent-magnet-manufacturing-plans/)
- **Defense primes:** the DFARS 252.225-7052 ban on Chinese, Russian, Iranian and North Korean content at any stage, from mining onward, takes effect **Jan 1, 2027**. It was already delayed one year from 2026, and contractors have lobbied for a second delay. In July 2026, Reuters reported that Pentagon suppliers (MP, USAR, Lynas, Energy Fuels, REalloys) said they cannot build enough capacity before the deadline, and that the administration is weighing extended access to some Chinese material. — [TechTimes, Oct 2, 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm); [OilPrice/Reuters, Jul 27, 2026](https://oilprice.com/Metals/Commodities/Pentagon-Suppliers-Warn-US-Wont-Have-Magnet-Capacity-By-2027.amp.html)

### Inferences
- OEM offtakes plus DoD floors and offtakes are what make US plants bankable. A Commerce view reported by REEx says domestic magnet production needs sustained commercial demand, not defense contracts alone. — [REEx, Sep 13, 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/us-ndfeb-magnet-supply-chain-china/)

## 5. Price premium of non-Chinese vs Chinese magnets

### Takeaway
No authoritative public benchmark exists for the price premium on ex-China finished magnets. The most defensible signal is oxide-level: in 2026, neodymium oxide traded about 58–63% higher ex-China (FOB or CIF Rotterdam) than inside China. The MP–DoW $110/kg NdPr floor sits well above Chinese domestic prices and is becoming a benchmark in offtake negotiations. Finished-magnet price ranges quoted by vendors conflict widely and are unreliable.

### Cited Findings
- March 10, 2026: Nd oxide at $113.05/kg inside China vs $184/kg FOB China, a 63% spread. — [rare-earth-mining.com](https://rare-earth-mining.com/rare-earth-market-pricing-analysis-march-2026/)
- September 2, 2026 (SMM): Nd oxide averaged $147/kg FOB China vs $232/kg CIF Rotterdam, a 58% gap. — [rare-earth-mining.com](https://rare-earth-mining.com/neodymium-price/)
- November 2025: Western buyers paying a 15–30% premium for non-Chinese rare earth supply. — [REEx deals newsletter](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/rare-earth-deals-financings-comments-on-trends-nov-14-21-2025/)
- Adamas: the MP–DoW floor "offers a new benchmark for the ex-China industry," which market participants confirm is factoring into offtake negotiations. Adamas expects allied price-floor harmonization in 2026, widening to Dy, Tb and Y. — [Adamas 2026 predictions](https://www.adamasintel.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Adamas-Intelligence-Rare-Earth-Magnet-Market-Outlook-Top-Predictions-for-2026.pdf)
- Vendor finished-magnet bands (unreliable, conflicting):
  - Ex-China high-grade $25–45+/kg; US $35–50+/kg. — [MAG SPRING](https://www.mag-spring.com/neodymium-iron-boron-ndfeb-magnet-price-guide/)

## 6. Bottlenecks: equipment, grain boundary diffusion (GBD) IP, heavy rare earths, labor, yield, qualification

### Takeaway
The binding constraints are less about magnet-forming lines than about (1) heavy rare earths: China refines more than 99% of Dy and Tb, and Lynas produced only 8 t of Dy+Tb in Q1 2026; (2) metal and alloy making; (3) Japanese and Chinese equipment with about 15-month lead times; and (4) customer qualification cycles. Workforce and yield data are scarce.

### Cited Findings
- **Adamas:** "With China dominating over 99% of refined dysprosium and terbium production… availability of these two critical elements outside China has potential to be a near-term bottleneck." Heavy rare earths and metal-making capacity are "near-term bottlenecks for the US magnet industry." — [Adamas 2026 predictions](https://www.adamasintel.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Adamas-Intelligence-Rare-Earth-Magnet-Market-Outlook-Top-Predictions-for-2026.pdf)
- Lynas reported 8 t of combined Dy+Tb output in Q1 2026. China processes about 99% of global heavy rare earths, and the US has 100% import reliance for them. — [TechTimes, Oct 2, 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- **Equipment (ULVAC IR Q&A, April 2026):**
  - One 3,000 tpa sintered line needs about 6–8 furnace units for melting, casting, sintering and aging, plus about 10 batch furnaces for GBD.
  - Lead time is about 1 year from order to shipment, plus 3–4 months for installation and start-up.
  - ULVAC is redesigning its Chigasaki factory as a dedicated melting-furnace plant "to meet demand, primarily from the U.S."
  - Source: [ULVAC IR Seminar 2026 Q&A](https://ir.ulvac.co.jp/en/ir/newsrelease/PressRelease2026040805/main/0/link/(EN)QA_for_IR_Seminer_2026.pdf)
- **Process chain and IP:** strip casting, hydrogen decrepitation, jet milling, field pressing, sintering, then heat treatment. Japanese firms, notably Hitachi Metals (now Proterial), held the core sintered patents, and Japan still controlled most of them as of 2019. Manufacturing is up to 30% of cost. Jet-mill oxygen above about 6,000 ppm degrades magnets. — [MacroPolo](https://archivemacropolo.org/analysis/permanent-magnets-case-study-industry-chinese-production-supply/); [patent US20150243433A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20150243433A1/en)
- **China's scale advantage:** JL MAG targets 60,000 tpa by 2027, and produced about 29,300 t of blanks in 2024. MP's full build-out equals about one-sixth of JL MAG's target. China held about 94% of global sintered magnet production in 2024 (IEA). — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm); [REEx](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/us-ndfeb-magnet-supply-chain-china/)
- **Qualification and feedstock:** REEx names qualification cycles and feedstock gaps as the main bottlenecks for MP, eVAC and Noveon. — [REEx, Sep 13, 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/us-ndfeb-magnet-supply-chain-china/)
  - MP and USAR were both still in qualification and pre-revenue stages for magnets in mid-2026. — [MP Q2](https://mpmaterials.com/news/mp-materials-reports-second-quarter-2026-results); [MarketBeat USAR](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/usa-rare-earth-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-10/)
  - USAR cited heavy rare earth input costs hurting gross margins. — [MarketBeat USAR](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/usa-rare-earth-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-08-10/)
- **Labor:** the US graduates only 200–250 mining engineers a year (State Department official David Copley, February 2026). The IEA estimates $60B of additional investment is needed by 2035. — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- Japan's imports of Chinese heavy rare earths fell about 80% in H1 2026. China still shipped finished magnets. — [Finviz/EMAT piece](https://finviz.com/news/389146/chinas-quiet-squeeze-on-japan-exposes-the-wests-magnet-blind-spot-nasdaq-emat)
- **Recycling as feed:** USAR projects swarf can supply up to 30% of future magnet rare earth oxide feed. — [USAR Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001970622/000197062226000056/exhibit991-earningsrelease.htm)

### Inferences
- **When could domestic supply cover US needs?** (synthesis)
  - **Defense NdFeB (DFARS-compliant):** forming capacity is probably adequate by 2027–28. Full mine-to-magnet compliance, especially Dy/Tb for high-coercivity grades and Sm for SmCo, is unlikely by Jan 1, 2027. A waiver or extension is likely, and suppliers themselves say capacity will be short. Realistic defense self-sufficiency is about 2028–2029, contingent on Mountain Pass, Wheat Ridge and Lynas Texas heavy rare earth separation, plus Serra Verde.
  - **Commercial direct demand** (about 10–20 kt): about 2029–2031 if MP 10X, USAR Blacksburg and Vulcan commission on schedule in 2028 and qualify within about 12–24 months. Equipment lead times (about 15 months) and heavy rare earth feed are the gating items.
  - **Total consumption including embedded imports** (about 40–50+ kt): not before the mid-2030s, if at all. REEx's base case has the US at about 10% of global sintered output only around 2031–33. — [REEx, Aug 25, 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/us-ndfeb-magnet-production-timeline/)
- GBD reduces Dy/Tb per magnet by about 50–70% (common industry knowledge, not sourced here) and is therefore strategically vital. Western GBD know-how and furnaces are mostly Japanese.

### Gaps

- No yield data for US plants.
- No specific workforce shortage data for magnet manufacturing.
- No information on Chinese strip-casting or jet-mill export restrictions to the US.
- DFARS waiver or extension status as of late October 2026 could not be confirmed because the search budget was exhausted.
