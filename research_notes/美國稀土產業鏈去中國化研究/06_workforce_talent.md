# Workforce and Human Capital Constraints in the US Rare Earth / Critical Minerals Supply Chain (as of Oct 22, 2026)

Scope note: ~25 searches/fetches. The original Reuters "A bachelor's in rare earths? In China, there are schools for that" (May 31 – Jun 1, 2026) could not be fetched in full (403/404); its findings are taken from syndicated search summaries (US News, mining.com, NST, Aaj) and from Rare Earth Exchanges, which restates them. Many "X times more" figures come from trade/advocacy commentary (Rare Earth Exchanges, Niskanen, DiscoveryAlert), not official statistics. They are labelled as such below.

## 1. US mining / metallurgy degree programs, graduates, and the retirement wave

### Takeaway
The US mining-engineering pipeline has shrunk from 25 programs (1982) to 14 accredited schools (2026). It now turns out roughly 160–330 degrees a year, depending on definition, against a DOE-estimated need of about 6,000 new mining-sector engineers over the next decade (~600 a year). That points to a supply gap of about 2x or more. About 221,000 mining workers (more than half the workforce) are expected to retire or need replacing by 2029.

### Cited Findings
**Programs and faculty**
- The number of US mining and mineral engineering programs fell from 25 in 1982 to 15 in 2023 — [CSIS, Thomas Hale, May 8, 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges). SME counts the accredited mining engineering programs as 25 (1982) falling to 14 (2020) — [SME technical briefing](https://www.smenet.org/What-We-Do/Technical-Briefings/Maintaining-the-Viability-of-U-S-Mining-Education). Tennessee State University's page gives 13 in 2023 — [TSU](https://www.tnstate.edu/cae/minor-in-mining-engineering.aspx). An older SME paper put the count at about 12 in 2012 — [SME 2013 PDF](https://www.smenet.org/docs/public/USMiningSchools-SME.pdf). The 2026 federal framing is "14 accredited U.S. mining schools" — [University of Arizona, Aug 10, 2026](https://news.arizona.edu/news/doe-invests-100m-us-mining-education); [Northern Miner, Aug 2026](https://www.northernminer.com/news/north-america-is-building-rare-earth-plants-faster-than-it-is-building-rare-earth-engineers/1003895179/).
- Faculty qualified to teach these subjects fell from about 120 (1984) to about 70 (2014), per SME data cited by [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges). SME gives about 120 (1984) to about 70 (2012) — [SME](https://www.smenet.org/What-We-Do/Technical-Briefings/Maintaining-the-Viability-of-U-S-Mining-Education).
- SME says the dissolution of the US Bureau of Mines removed all funding for mining schools under the Mining and Mineral Resource Institutes Act of 1984, and that programs suffer from dwindling federal R&D funding — [SME](https://www.smenet.org/What-We-Do/Technical-Briefings/Maintaining-the-Viability-of-U-S-Mining-Education).

**Graduates. Counts conflict because definitions differ (BS only vs. all degree levels, mining vs. mining + metallurgy).**
- 2020: 327 degrees were awarded in mining and mineral engineering, a 39% net drop in graduations since 2016. The average graduate salary was $115,761 — [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges).
- 2023: 162 US mining engineering graduates, against 400–600 needed each year — [Niskanen Center, Aug 14, 2025](https://www.niskanencenter.org/building-a-domestic-critical-mineral-workforce-through-immigration/). "Just over 200" mining and metallurgical engineering degrees in 2023 (SME data) — [Rare Earth Exchanges, Jul 15, 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/the-west-is-fighting-a-talent-war-it-doesnt-yet-know-its-losing/).
- 2024: 285 mining and mineral engineering degrees were awarded nationally, 60 of them by Colorado School of Mines (Data USA, as reported by Reuters) — [Reuters via US News, Jun 1, 2026](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-01/a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that).
- SME reports graduates down about 43% since 2015. Secondary reporting gives about 600 mining engineering students enrolled in 2022, down from 1,500 in 2015 — [SME](https://www.smenet.org/What-We-Do/Technical-Briefings/Maintaining-the-Viability-of-U-S-Mining-Education); [GBR](https://www.gbreports.com/article/usa-the-role-of-universities-in-the-minings-future) (secondary; verify).
- US metallurgy enrollment is "only a few thousand students," while China graduates "tens of thousands" a year — [Niskanen 2025](https://www.niskanencenter.org/building-a-domestic-critical-mineral-workforce-through-immigration/) (approximate, unsourced magnitude).

**Demand and the retirement wave**
- DOE estimates the US will need about 6,000 new mining-sector engineers over the next decade. It also cites shortages in materials science, processing and recycling — [DOE press release, Aug 7, 2026](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-launches-100-million-initiative-build-americas-critical-minerals).
- More than half of the US mining workforce, about 221,000 workers, must be retired and replaced by 2029 — [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges); SME estimate restated in [UArizona, Aug 2026](https://news.arizona.edu/news/doe-invests-100m-us-mining-education). An earlier SME projection: 21% (128,000) retiring by 2019 and 52% (221,000) by 2029 — [SME Workforce Trends](https://www.smenet.org/What-We-Do/Technical-Briefings/Workforce-Trends-in-the-US-Mining-Industry).
- The mining workforce declined 20.4% over the past decade — [Niskanen 2025](https://www.niskanencenter.org/building-a-domestic-critical-mineral-workforce-through-immigration/).
- Over 70% of polled 15–30-year-olds said they "definitely" or "probably would not" consider mining. 86% of mining executives said recruiting is harder than two years earlier — [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges).
- Peer countries are also short: Canada projects 80,000–120,000 missing mining workers by 2030; Australia needs 24,400 new workers by 2026 against about 16,000 supplied — [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges).
- Universities report strong placement: University of Arizona mining engineering graduates have 100% job placement — [UArizona 2026](https://news.arizona.edu/news/doe-invests-100m-us-mining-education).

### Inferences
- If ~285 degrees a year (2024) is the right base, the DOE need of ~600 a year implies about a 2x shortfall. That is roughly what PROSPECT's "double graduates in two years" goal targets. Even at 2x, US output would stay at about 1/10 or less of China's mining graduate output (see Section 2).
- Mining engineers are not the tightest constraint for rare earths. Mountain Pass is one mine. The real bottleneck is the midstream (separation chemists, solvent-extraction operators, metallurgists) and downstream magnet technicians, where the US has no dedicated degree pipeline at all.
- Labor is tight across allied countries (Canada, Australia), which limits how much "friend-shoring" of talent is possible.

### Gaps
- No NSF/NCES series back to the 1980s for extractive or metallurgical engineering specifically was retrieved. The 1980s graduate counts (reportedly far higher, on the order of 700+ a year) were not confirmed in this session.
- Metallurgical/materials engineering and chemical engineering graduate counts (NCES CIP 14.20 / 14.07) were not pulled. Rare Earth Exchanges estimates US midstream-relevant graduates (chemistry, ChemE, materials/metallurgy) at about 50,000–85,000 a year (see Section 2), but that is an estimate.

## 2. China's rare earth workforce, R&D scale, and the "X times more" claims

### Takeaway
China has an institutionalized rare-earth talent system with no Western equivalent. It runs 41+ dedicated rare-earth labs and institutes, at least 11 universities and colleges with rare-earth degree programs enrolling 500+ students a year, and roughly 3,000 to tens of thousands of mining graduates a year. Best estimates put "several thousand" practical separation experts in China against about 100–250 in the US (and only a few dozen across the US, EU and Japan by stricter definitions). Magnet-manufacturing hands are estimated at 8,000–15,000 in China against 300–900 in the US. Depending on the segment, that is a gap of about 10x to 50x+.

### Cited Findings
**Institutions and degree programs (Reuters investigation, May 31 – Jun 1, 2026)**
- China has an ecosystem of more than 40 specialist rare-earth laboratories (Reuters counted 41 dedicated labs and institutes, many near mines). It also has at least 11 universities and technical colleges that together enroll more than 500 students a year in rare-earth degree programs. Reuters could not identify any school outside China offering an undergraduate rare-earth degree — [Reuters via US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-01/a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that); [mining.com syndication](https://www.mining.com/web/insight-a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that/); [NST](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/opinion/columnists/2026/06/1453697/bachelors-rare-earths-china-there-are-schools).
- Jiangxi University of Science & Technology's new rare-earth degree will take in 70 students (state media). It covers processing and metallurgy through magnets and includes company research projects. Inner Mongolia University (of Science & Technology) rare-earth majors get more than 100 hours of rare-earth-specific courses — [Reuters syndication](https://english.aaj.tv/news/amp/330459729); [Rare Earth Exchanges, Jul 15, 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/the-west-is-fighting-a-talent-war-it-doesnt-yet-know-its-losing/).
- Reuters had earlier reported only "a couple of dozen" rare-earth separation and refining experts in the US, Europe and Japan combined, against thousands in China — [Reuters syndication summary](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-01/a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that). Niskanen gives the same figure as "a few dozen" — [Niskanen 2025](https://www.niskanencenter.org/building-a-domestic-critical-mineral-workforce-through-immigration/).

**Mining and mineral processing schools**
- China has over 38 mineral processing schools and upwards of 44 mining engineering programs. Central South University alone has 1,000 undergraduates and 500 graduate students in mineral processing — [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges).
- China produces about 3,000 mining engineering graduates a year from 45 programs, against 162 in the US (2023), about 18x — [Niskanen 2025](https://www.niskanencenter.org/building-a-domestic-critical-mineral-workforce-through-immigration/). Other commentary gives about 10,000 Chinese mining engineers a year against about 200 in the US, described as approximate — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/rare-earths-greatest-shortage-isnt-capital-its-people/). DiscoveryAlert puts US output (~285) at about 1/15 of China's — [DiscoveryAlert](https://discoveryalert.com.au/rare-earth-talent-shortage-us-processing-workforce-crisis/) (aggregator).

**Rare Earth Exchanges' "best estimates" (Jul 15, 2026; self-described "reasoned estimates, not official counts", medium-low confidence)** — [source](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/the-west-is-fighting-a-talent-war-it-doesnt-yet-know-its-losing/)

| Segment | China | US | Implied ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practical RE separation expertise (people) | "several thousand" | ~100–250 | ~10–40x+ |
| Hands-on permanent-magnet manufacturing (people) | ~8,000–15,000 | ~300–900 | ~10–50x |
| Dedicated rare-earth majors (students/yr) | 500+ | 0 undergraduate majors | n/a |
| Mining & metallurgical engineering grads/yr | ~20,000–40,000 | ~200 UG; <500 incl. graduate | ~40–100x+ |
| Midstream grads/yr (chemistry, ChemE, materials) | 300,000–600,000 | ~50,000–85,000 | ~4–10x |
| Downstream grads/yr (ME, EE, controls) | ~0.8–1.2 million | ~90,000–150,000 | ~6–10x |

- Another claim: fewer than 10 people in the US can competently design a heavy rare earth separation plant — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/us-mining-engineers-china-rare-earth-workforce/) (unverified).

**Regional R&D scale (Baotou)**
- Baotou National Rare-Earth High-tech Zone (Apr 2025): 377 high-level talents, 3,228 full-time R&D staff at enterprise R&D centers, 89 academicians and experts brought in (flexible arrangements), 207 innovation platforms, and 400+ high-tech SMEs — [China Services Info, Apr 17, 2025](https://www.chinaservicesinfo.com/s/202504/17/WS681b1838498eec7e1f7361f4/baotou-accelerates-rare-earth-industrial-development.html).
- 7 national-level rare-earth R&D platforms are built on the Baotou Research Institute of Rare Earths, including the National Engineering Research Center for RE Metallurgy & Functional Materials, the State Key Lab of Bayan Obo RE Resources, and the National RE Functional Materials Innovation Center — [Inner Mongolia Dept. of Science & Technology](https://kjt.nmg.gov.cn/kjdt/mscx/202308/t20230831_2370694.html?zbb=true).

**Formalizing the technician pipeline**
- China drafted a national occupational standard, "Rare Earth Materials Production Technician." It is led by China Northern Rare Earth Group, has passed final review, and awaits implementation (reported Jun 5, 2026, citing state-affiliated media) — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/china-moves-to-formalize-rare-earth-workforce-standards-as-industry-matures/).

**China restricting its own experts (human-capital export controls)**
- The WSJ reported (late June 2025) that MOFCOM asked rare-earth companies for lists of specialist employees, including expertise, education and research background, to catalog and monitor experts. Some experts were asked to hand passports to employers or local authorities. The lists cover upstream processing staff and downstream magnet staff — [TipRanks/TheFly summary of WSJ](https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/china-asks-rare-earth-companies-for-information-on-experts-wsj-reports-thefly); [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/?p=41397); [TechNews (Chinese)](https://technews.tw/2025/06/26/china-is-tracking-down-its-rare-earth-experts-and-taking-away-passports/). The reporting relies on anonymous sources, and there was no official Chinese confirmation.
- Mugglehead ties this to China's December 2023 ban on exporting rare-earth extraction and separation technology — [Mugglehead](https://mugglehead.com/china-reportedly-pulling-passports-from-rare-earth-experts-over-national-security-fears).
- Niskanen notes that China has "cracked down on Chinese mining experts working for foreign companies" (no figures) — [Niskanen 2025](https://www.niskanencenter.org/building-a-domestic-critical-mineral-workforce-through-immigration/).

### Inferences
- The gap is widest where de-sinicization is hardest: separation (SX) and magnet sintering. Mining-engineer ratios (~18x) understate it. For separation know-how, the most credible framing is "thousands vs. low hundreds" (US) or "a few dozen" (all of the West, by a strict expert-level definition).
- China's passport and expert-catalog measures, plus the 2023 technology export ban, close off the historical route by which Western plants got started (hiring or contracting Chinese engineers). That makes the Western pipeline more of a binding constraint.
- Chinese headcount comes with tacit knowledge accumulated over decades (process recipes, SX cascade tuning). Degree counts alone understate this advantage.

### Gaps
- No official MIIT/MOE totals for China's rare-earth workforce or R&D personnel were found. The Baotou Research Institute's own staff count was not found. Northeastern University and other programs' rare-earth enrollments were not quantified.
- The Reuters 2026 article's full text (executive quotes, Western company examples) could not be retrieved.
- Whether China's Oct 2025 export-control expansions explicitly cover technical services and personnel (e.g., overseas assistance or maintenance of RE lines) was not verified in this session.

## 3. Company-level shortages, hiring, training, and reliance on foreign expertise

### Takeaway
US projects announce thousands of jobs: MP 10X (1,500+), Vulcan Benson (1,000), e-VAC (300), USA Rare Earth Stillwater. Actual current headcounts are small (MP Fort Worth ~160+ in 2025; Noveon ~100 to a few hundred). MP's 2017–2018 restart drew on Chinese (Shenghe) technical know-how, and companies now rely on in-house training, state programs (readySC) and digital onboarding tools to close the skills gap.

### Cited Findings
**MP Materials**
- The Mountain Pass restart took 18 months "and technical help from the Chinese" — [Forbes Middle East, 2024](https://db.westchesterlibraries.org:2090/uae/forbes-middle-east/20240901/281689735200821). Reuters reported that the DOE told government scientists not to collaborate with MP because it was about 10% owned by a Chinese investor (Shenghe) and "relies heavily on Chinese sales and technical know-how, according to the company" (undated in the result; circa 2019–2020) — [Reuters via aflo](https://www.aflo.com/ja/contents/126047030).
- MP employed more than 160 people in Fort Worth (scientists, engineers, production technicians and operators) as of 2025 — [Hillwood](https://www.hillwood.com/newsroom/news-articles/mp-materials-to-build-1-25-billion-rare-earth-magnet-factory-near-fort-worth/).
- The 10X campus in Northlake, TX ($1.25B, announced Feb 2026) is projected at more than 1,500 jobs, with testing starting in 2028 — [Hillwood, Feb 26, 2026](https://www.hillwood.com/newsroom/news-articles/mp-materials-to-build-1-25-billion-rare-earth-magnet-factory-near-fort-worth/); [Trade & Industry Development](https://www.tradeandindustrydev.com/region/texas/news/tx-mp-materials-expand-rare-earth-magnet-35573.md).
- The Apple–MP deal (Jul 15, 2025, $500M) includes a joint training commitment to build "an entirely new pool of U.S. talent and expertise in magnet manufacturing" — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250715345597/en/Apple-expands-U.S.-supply-chain-with-500-million-commitment-to-American-rare-earth-magnets); [Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/apple-signs-500m-deal-rare-earth-magnet-supplier-mp-materials/753153).
- One aggregator counted 102 open MP roles as of Aug 31, 2026, concentrated in specialized engineering — [MigrateMate](https://migratemate.co/companies-hiring/mp-materials) (third-party).

**USA Rare Earth (Stillwater, OK)**
- Phase 1a magnet line commissioned, with customer orders from Q2 2026. Run-rate is targeted at 600 t/yr by end-2026 and 1,200 t/yr with Phase 1b by Q1 2027. The company spent 2025 "hiring and training engineers and technicians." It acquired UK-based Less Common Metals (metal/alloy know-how) in Nov 2025 — [Nasdaq/Zacks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/will-stillwater-facility-ramp-drive-long-term-growth-usar); [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2881833/usar-gains-627-in-the-past-year-buy-sell-or-hold-the-stock). These are secondary sources with no headcount given.

**e-VAC Magnetics (VAC subsidiary, Sumter, SC)**
- $506M investment and 300 jobs (announced Dec 2023); operations were planned for late 2025. Recruiting and training run through SC Technical College System's readySC program — [SC Daily Gazette, Dec 13, 2023](https://scdailygazette.com/2023/12/13/german-manufacturer-to-invest-506m-hire-300-workers-in-sumter/); [Area Development](https://www.areadevelopment.com/newsitems/12-15-2023/e-vac-magnetics-sumter-south-carolina.shtml).

**Noveon Magnetics (San Marcos, TX)**
- Noveon says it increased its workforce fourfold in a year and partnered with DeepHow (AI video training) in 2026 to "close the skilled workforce gap in U.S. magnet production" — [01net syndicated release](https://www.01net.it/noveon-magnetics-partners-with-deephow-to-close-the-skilled-workforce-gap-in-u-s-magnet-production/). Earlier headcount: 45 to 65 (2022), and "25 to nearly 100" in other coverage — [Noveon/Fast Company](https://noveon.co/fastcompanynoveon); [Noveon](https://noveon.co/san-marcos-company-aims-to-energize-us-magnets-supply). It relies on nearby Texas State University — [Hays Caldwell EDP](https://hayscaldwelledp.com/site-consultants/case-studies/urban-mining).

**Vulcan Elements (Benson, NC)**
- A $1B plant for 10,000 t magnet capacity and 1,000 jobs, funded by a $620M OSC loan, $50M Commerce (CHIPS) and $550M private capital. The CEO said the site was chosen for engineering talent and that it seeks "PhDs, engineers and technicians from complementary industries." Job postings require "US persons" because the work is export-controlled — [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/magnet-maker-grows-as-us-seeks-rare-earths-amid-china-trade-war); [Mining Weekly, Nov 19, 2025](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/rare-earth-magnet-startup-vulcan-elements-to-build-1bn-north-carolina-plant-2025-11-19); [startup.jobs posting](https://startup.jobs/manufacturing-engineer-vulcan-elements-8106949).

**Neo Performance Materials**
- Neo's partnership with Tallinn University of Technology (Estonia; Narva magnet plant) is described as the only formal rare-earth talent pipeline found outside China — [Reuters summary via search](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-01/a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that).

### Inferences
- Announced US magnet and processing jobs add up to roughly 3,000+ (MP 10X 1,500 + Vulcan 1,000 + e-VAC 300 + USAR + Noveon) by about 2028–2029. That is several times the estimated current US base of 300–900 hands-on magnet workers. Most would need to be trained from adjacent industries (semiconductors, powder metallurgy, chemicals).
- "US persons" requirements for export-controlled (defense) work narrow the hiring pool and rule out much foreign-national hiring, including Chinese nationals.
- The industry has been moving away from Chinese technical help (MP after Shenghe) toward internal know-how plus acquisitions of non-Chinese know-how (USAR–LCM).

### Gaps
- No verified current headcounts for MP Mountain Pass or Fort Worth (2026), USAR, e-VAC (operational status unconfirmed), or Noveon in 2026.
- No wage data for SX operators or magnet technicians were found. No earnings-call quotes on staffing bottlenecks were retrieved.
- Lynas Texas (Seadrift) staffing plans were not researched. The project's status is uncertain.

## 4. Government and industry workforce initiatives

### Takeaway
Federal action stepped up in Aug–Sep 2026: $180M in mining-education grants (DOE PROSPECT $100M plus Department of War $80M to three schools), a $16M PROSPECT Planning Prize, and a Mining Schools Act provision in the House-passed FY2027 NDAA (Sec. 1883). Skilled-immigration fixes have not been enacted. These measures are small next to the gap and only begin to graduate people around 2028–2030.

### Cited Findings
- **DOE PROSPECT** (Providing Opportunities for Specialized Education in Critical Technologies), Aug 7, 2026: a Notice of Intent for up to $100M from the Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation. The goal is to double mining, minerals and supply-chain graduates within two years, along with curricula, teaching tools and financial aid — [DOE, Aug 7, 2026](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-launches-100-million-initiative-build-americas-critical-minerals). The aim is to double graduates at the 14 accredited mining schools — [UArizona, Aug 10, 2026](https://news.arizona.edu/news/doe-invests-100m-us-mining-education).
- **Department of War:** $80M to Colorado School of Mines, South Dakota School of Mines and Johns Hopkins, for a total of $180M with DOE — [UArizona 2026](https://news.arizona.edu/news/doe-invests-100m-us-mining-education); [Rep. Biggs](https://biggs.house.gov/media/press-releases/white-house-advances-congressman-biggss-effort-strengthen-americas-mining).
- **PROSPECT Planning Prize:** $16M total, up to $1M per winner, launched Sep 14, 2026, deadline Oct 5, 2026. It is open to programs for early-career and incumbent workers, educators, and university, community-college and high-school students — [DOE CMEI](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/articles/energy-department-launches-16-million-prize-grow-mining-and-critical-minerals); [Battery Tech Online](https://www.batterytechonline.com/battery-manufacturing/-16-million-prize-to-boost-us-critical-minerals-workforce).
- **NDAA:** Core provisions of Rep. Burgess Owens's Mining Schools Act (competitive DOE grants to mining schools) are in the House-passed FY2027 NDAA (216–212, Jul 22, 2026; Sec. 1883). No Senate action had been confirmed as of Aug 2026 — [Owens](https://owens.house.gov/posts/owens-secures-core-provisions-of-mining-schools-act-in-ndaa); [Deseret News, Jul 23, 2026](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2026/07/23/owens-mining-education-bill/); [Northern Miner](https://www.northernminer.com/news/north-america-is-building-rare-earth-plants-faster-than-it-is-building-rare-earth-engineers/1003895179/).
- **Critical Mineral Mining Education Act** (H.R.7021 / S.3612): fellowship and visiting-scholar international exchange programs at $10M a year for 10 years. It had not moved since January 2026 — [Sen. Scott](https://www.scott.senate.gov/?p=21707); [Baltimore Fishbowl](https://baltimorefishbowl.com/stories/olszewski-rosen-push-bipartisan-bill-to-strengthen-u-s-mining-workforce/); [Kaine/WVVA, Jan 14, 2026](https://www.wvva.com/2026/01/14/kaine-pushes-bill-compete-with-china-critical-minerals/). An earlier bill, S.912 (2023, Barrasso/Manchin), proposed technology grants for mining education — [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges).
- **Colorado School of Mines** is developing two new critical-minerals research facilities with DOE; the first is expected to open in 2027 — [Reuters syndication](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-01/a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that).
- **University of Arizona:** MiningWerx hub, the San Xavier Underground Mining Lab, $850K from the state (Oct 2025) and $3M federal (Feb 2026) for a mineral-processing plant, with industry partners Komatsu, Resolution Copper and South32 — [UArizona 2026](https://news.arizona.edu/news/doe-invests-100m-us-mining-education).
- **State programs:** South Carolina's readySC recruits and trains workers for e-VAC — [SC Daily Gazette](https://scdailygazette.com/2023/12/13/german-manufacturer-to-invest-506m-hire-300-workers-in-sumter/). Texas and local governments offered about $200M in incentives for MP 10X — [Plant Services](https://www.plantservices.com/industry-news/news/55361522/mp-materials-to-build-125-billion-rare-earth-magnet-expansion).
- **Immigration:** 12.5% of mining-industry workers and 23.7% of petroleum, mining and geological engineers are foreign-born. Niskanen proposes EB-2 national-interest waivers for critical-minerals engineers and a bipartisan "Critical Minerals Workforce Enhancement Act." It notes peers already act: Canada's Rural and Northern Immigration Pilot, Australia's priority skilled list, and South Africa's critical-skills visa — [Niskanen, Aug 14, 2025](https://www.niskanencenter.org/building-a-domestic-critical-mineral-workforce-through-immigration/). I could not find independent confirmation of that bill's status.
- **Rare Earth Exchanges** recommends two-year technician credentials in SX, sintering and QA — [REE, Jul 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/the-west-is-fighting-a-talent-war-it-doesnt-yet-know-its-losing/) (opinion).

### Inferences
- $180M spread over 14+ schools is meaningful against the historic near-zero, but small against China's state-run system. Programs funded in late 2026 add capacity for students graduating around 2028–2030 (2-year technicians) and around 2030–2031 (4-year BS). Experienced plant leadership takes longer.
- Federal programs focus on mining and minerals degrees. None is a dedicated rare-earth separation or magnet curriculum like China's. The midstream gap is still largely left to employers.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session: Critical Materials Innovation Hub (Ames Lab) training programs, Montana Tech, Penn State, Texas/Oklahoma community-college rare-earth programs (e.g., around USAR Stillwater or MP Fort Worth), and DOL registered apprenticeships in magnets.
- Whether the PROSPECT $100M FOA has formally opened, and who won the prize, is not yet known.
- The final FY2027 NDAA workforce text, which depends on Senate and conference action, is unknown as of Oct 2026.

## 5. Knowledge transfer from Japan and Europe

### Takeaway
Knowledge transfer from Japan and Europe into the US happens mostly through corporate vehicles (VAC/e-VAC's German magnet know-how in South Carolina, USAR's purchase of UK-based LCM, Neo's Estonian pipeline). In this session I found no documented personnel or know-how transfer from Shin-Etsu, Proterial (Hitachi Metals) or Solvay into US plants.

### Cited Findings
- VAC (Germany) entered the US through e-VAC in Sumter, SC, carrying its own sintered-magnet process. Local hiring runs through readySC — [Area Development](https://www.areadevelopment.com/newsitems/12-15-2023/e-vac-magnetics-sumter-south-carolina.shtml); [metal-powder.tech](https://www.metal-powder.tech/?p=277818).
- USA Rare Earth acquired Less Common Metals (UK metal and alloy producer) in Nov 2025 to supply Stillwater — [Zacks/Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/will-stillwater-facility-ramp-drive-long-term-growth-usar).
- Neo Performance Materials and Tallinn University of Technology run what is reportedly the only formal rare-earth talent pipeline outside China — [Reuters syndication](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-01/a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that).
- Lynas, Australia's analog: labor costs are up to 4x higher in Australia than in Malaysia, and water up to 10x — [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnews.com.au/node/402249). This shows how Western siting raises costs even for an experienced non-Chinese operator.

### Inferences
- The non-Chinese pool of experienced RE separation and magnet experts is small. By Reuters' strict count it is a couple of dozen separation experts across the US, EU and Japan. Japanese magnet makers guard their process know-how. So Japan and Europe can seed leadership teams but cannot staff US plants at scale.

### Gaps
- No sources found on Shin-Etsu, Proterial or TDK US magnet investments or personnel secondments, on Solvay La Rochelle staff supporting US projects, or on JOGMEC/Japanese involvement in US workforce development.

## 6. Time to build a workforce at scale; labor costs and competitiveness

### Takeaway
Building a trained workforce takes about 2 years for technicians, 4–6 years for engineers, and about 10+ years for senior process experts able to design and debottleneck SX cascades. Western labor costs (up to about 4x the Asian level, by the Lynas analog) push plants toward automation. Human capital is therefore a binding constraint, especially on separation and heavy-RE timelines through about 2030, but not an absolute barrier to the first-wave magnet plants.

### Cited Findings
- DOE's PROSPECT targets doubling graduates "within two years," which is itself a multi-year horizon — [DOE, Aug 2026](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-launches-100-million-initiative-build-americas-critical-minerals).
- Separation is technically demanding: 17 rare earths with nearly identical chemistry — [Reuters syndication](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-06-01/a-bachelors-in-rare-earths-in-china-there-are-schools-for-that). A 2012 US separation plant design (Avalon, Louisiana) had a solvent extraction circuit of more than 1,000 mixer-settlers, the largest cost item in a $302M plant — [mining.com](https://www.mining.com/?p=307460).
- An unverified secondary report says many Chinese SX plants use more than 200 workers manually adjusting valves and flows. Western demonstration plants (e.g., Saskatchewan) use AI and advanced process control to offset labor — [aggregator post](https://scribe.disroot.org/comment/9785934) (low reliability).
- Lynas: Australian labor costs are up to 4x higher than overseas (Malaysia) — [Business News Australia](https://www.businessnews.com.au/node/402249).
- The average US mining and mineral engineering graduate salary is $115,761 (2020 data) — [CSIS 2023](https://www.csis.org/analysis/united-states-needs-more-mining-engineers-solve-its-critical-mineral-challenges).
- MP 10X testing starts in 2028; Vulcan Benson and USAR Phase 1b ramp in 2027 — [Hillwood](https://www.hillwood.com/newsroom/news-articles/mp-materials-to-build-1-25-billion-rare-earth-magnet-factory-near-fort-worth/); [Nasdaq/Zacks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/will-stillwater-facility-ramp-drive-long-term-growth-usar).

### Inferences
- **Binding-constraint assessment.**
  - Mining engineers: a moderate constraint, partly offset by flows from other mining sectors.
  - Separation and refining chemists and SX operators: a binding constraint. The pool is only low hundreds, China blocks the flow of its experts, and there is no degree pipeline.
  - Magnet technicians: a moderate-to-binding constraint. They can be trained in 1–2 years from adjacent manufacturing, but the plants announced for 2027–2029 need several times today's base.
  - Senior process designers (especially heavy RE): binding. One estimate puts them at fewer than 10 people.
- Human capital likely adds a 2–5-year lag beyond capital and permitting timelines for separation and heavy-RE capacity. That is consistent with commissioning-to-nameplate ramps of several years at Western plants. This is an inference, not a sourced estimate.
- Labor-cost disadvantage (~3–4x) is secondary to scale and know-how. Plants offset it through automation and digital training (Noveon–DeepHow) and through price floors and offtakes (DoD–MP). Labor cost alone does not decide US competitiveness, but it raises opex in labor-intensive SX.

### Gaps
- No like-for-like US vs. China wage data for SX operators or magnet technicians. No sourced "time-to-competency" figures for SX operators. No BLS occupational data specific to rare-earth processing.
