## 1. China's share by segment (2024–2026), consolidation, quotas and feedstock imports

### Takeaway
China still holds about 60–69% of rare earth mining, 85–91% of refining/separation, about 99% of heavy rare earth (HREE) separation and about 94% of sintered NdFeB magnet output. 2025 was the first year China's refining share fell noticeably, from 90% to 85%, driven by MP Materials (US) and Lynas (Malaysia). Downstream stages (metals, alloys, magnets) have barely diversified.

### Cited Findings
**Mining**
- In 2024 China produced 60% of global mined *magnet* rare earths. The top three producers held 86% of mining. — [IEA, *Rare Earth Elements: Pathways to secure and diversified supply chains* (2026), p.~30](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)
- USGS MCS 2026, as relayed by secondary summaries: China about 270,000 t of 390,000 t REO in 2025 (69.2%), US about 51,000 t (13.1%), Myanmar 9.7%, Australia 4.6%. A USGS open-file report on China (June 2026) puts China at 255,000 t (68%). One secondary source gives the US figure as 45,000 t, so the exact numbers are unverified. — [USGS MCS 2026 heavy REE chapter](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-rare-earths-heavy.pdf); [USGS OFR 2026-1018](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20261018/full); [Farmonaut summary (secondary)](https://farmonaut.com/mining/china-rare-earth-production-share-2025-2026-global-mining)
- Reserves: China about 44 Mt, Brazil 21 Mt, Malaysia 16.1 Mt (third largest). — [CSIS, Baskaran & Schwartz, "Rare Earth Export Restrictions One Year Later," Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- IEA base case: China's share of mined magnet REE falls from about 60% (2025) to 55% (2035), and to 45% in the high-production case if every announced project is delivered on time. — [IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026, p.181](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)

**Refining/separation**
- China had 91% of global refined output in 2024. The top three countries had 97%. — [IEA REE report (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)
- "China's share dropped from 90% to 85% between 2024 and 2025, with the majority of the lost share being claimed by the United States and the rest by Malaysia." IEA projects 73% in 2035 (base case) and 70% (high-production case). Rare earths were "the notable exception" to rising refining concentration in 2025. — [IEA GCMO 2026, pp.9, 181–182](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- The IEA GCMO 2026 export-restriction table lists rare earths at a 91% China share. — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)

**Heavy REE separation**
- "China processes about 99% of the world's heavy rare earth supply". US import reliance for HREE is 100%. — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- Mining.com's coverage of Baskaran says China still controls about 90% of global HREE separation. Baskaran argues that cutting it to about half would leave the US and its allies much stronger. — [MINING.COM, "China's rare earth clampdown backfired: CSIS analyst" (mid-2026)](https://www.mining.com/chinas-rare-earth-clampdown-backfired-csis-analyst/). *Conflict:* the 99% vs 90% figures probably reflect different definitions (all HREE vs Dy/Tb separation capacity).
- Lynas produced only 8 t of Dy+Tb combined in Q1 2026, against defense demand of "thousands of metric tons annually". — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)

**Magnets**
- China's share of sintered permanent magnet production rose from about 50% in 2005 to 94% in 2024. Its share of Dy/Tb-containing NdFeB was about 94% in 2024. — [IEA REE report (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)
- 94% of magnet-production scrap is generated in China, which limits recycling feedstock elsewhere. — [IEA REE report (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)
- JL MAG, the largest magnet maker by volume in 2024, targets 60,000 t/yr by 2027. That is about six times MP Materials' full planned US build-out of about 10,000 t/yr. — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)

**Consolidation and quotas**
- Only two state groups, China Rare Earth Group and China Northern Rare Earth, were eligible for mining quotas (down from six). The 2025 quotas were issued quietly and late. The first 2025 batch reportedly went to Northern RE, China RE Group and likely Shenghe on 9 July 2025. — [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/exclusivechina-quietly-issues-2025-rare-earth-quotas-sources-say-4141398); [The Rare Earth Observer](https://treo.substack.com/p/re-prices-go-apesht-after-quota-the)
- China Rare Earth Group was formed in 2022 by merging existing Chinese rare earth firms. — [Global Witness](https://globalwitness.org/en/campaigns/transition-minerals/fuelling-the-future-poisoning-the-present-myanmars-rare-earth-boom/)

**Feedstock imports (Myanmar/Laos)**
- China's HREE oxide imports from Myanmar rose from 19,500 t (2021) to 41,700 t (2023), more than double China's own domestic HREE mining quota. — [Global Witness](https://globalwitness.org/en/campaigns/transition-minerals/fuelling-the-future-poisoning-the-present-myanmars-rare-earth-boom/)
- Jan–Sep 2025: Myanmar exported USD 624M (more than 28,000 t) of rare earths to China. Of China's more than 52,000 t of REE imports in that period, 53% came from Myanmar. Border closures began in late 2024 and reopened gradually, with four KIA-controlled gates reopened in late Oct 2025. The Kachin Independence Organisation consolidated control of mining zones (Mar 2026). — [ISP-Myanmar](https://ispmyanmar.com/pet-03/); [Kachin News, 20 Mar 2026](https://kachinnews.com/2026/03/20/myanmars-rare-earths-and-the-quiet-loss-of-strategic-supply-chain-leverage/); [CNBC, 24 Jun 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/24/chinas-rare-earth-dominance-myanmar-plays-a-critical-role-.html)
- China Rare Earth Group reportedly acquired an option on a Lao REE refinery. — [The Rare Earth Observer (headline only)](https://treo.substack.com/p/crec-acquire-option-on-lao-re-refinery?open=false)
- The IEA names "sustained social and governance challenges in Myanmar" as a tangible supply risk. — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.182](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)

**Chinese export volume in 2026**
- China's rare earth exports fell 9.6% to 43,711 t in Jan–Sep 2026. September alone was up 6.7% year on year. — [technology.org, 19 Oct 2026 (via search summary; direct fetch blocked)](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/19/china-rare-earth-export-pause-november-10/)

### Inferences
- The concentration stack is steepest at the downstream end: about 60–69% at the mine, 85–91% at refining, about 94% for magnets and about 99% for HREE. This is why "mining independence" is a misleading metric. The US can already mine more light REE than it uses, but cannot yet turn most of it into Dy/Tb-doped magnets.
- China's control of HREE is partly *offshore*: it relies on Myanmar (and increasingly Laos) ionic-clay feed. Its HREE dominance is therefore really a processing and offtake monopoly more than a geological one. That point cuts both ways for the US: other geology exists, but China's processing lock is the binding constraint.

### Gaps
- I could not access the official 2026 Chinese quota figures or the full-year 2025 Chinese import totals.
- Primary USGS MCS 2026 tables were not opened, so the US mine output figure (45 kt vs 51 kt) is unconfirmed.

## 2. China's countermeasure toolkit and timeline (2010 to Oct 2026) and effects on US companies

### Takeaway
China has moved from informal embargoes (2010) to a formal, layered licensing system. The layers are: an element-specific licensing regime (Apr 2025, still in force); an extraterritorial 0.1% de minimis and technology package (Oct 2025, suspended); entity listing of US rare-earth companies (Jun 2026); a whistleblower/enforcement mechanism (Jul 2026); and military end-user bans (Japan, Jan 2026). The Busan truce suspended only the October 2025 package. China now applies the April 2025 regime as "a dial, not a switch": selective, slow licences that hit US and defense users hardest.

### Cited Findings
**Chronology**
- **2010:** After the Senkaku boat collision, China was accused of an informal rare earth export ban on Japan, which it denied. **2012:** The US, EU and Japan filed a WTO case. **2014:** The WTO ruled against China. **Jan 2015:** China dropped its export quotas. — [Wikipedia, Rare earths trade dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earths_trade_dispute)
- **Since about 2008:** China has restricted exports of key production technology. — [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon)
- **Dec 2023:** China banned exports of rare earth processing (extraction/separation) and magnet-making technology. — [CSIS, "What China's Ban on Rare Earths Processing Technology Exports Means"](https://www.csis.org/analysis/what-chinas-ban-rare-earths-processing-technology-exports-means) (the month is from the article's framing). The IEA notes that China's 2023 controls "covered a wide range of rare earth processing equipment and technologies". — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.287](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- **2023–2024 germanium/gallium:** Germanium controls began under MOFCOM/GAC Announcement No. 23 of 2023. They moved into the Dual-Use Items Export Control List under MOFCOM Announcement No. 51 of 2024, effective 1 Dec 2024. — [Morgan Lewis, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/recent-china-export-control-actions-signal-active-enforcement-for-rare-earths-and-strategic-minerals). China restricted gallium, rare earths and at least seven other materials between 2023 and 2026. — [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon)
- **4 Apr 2025:** Licensing imposed on seven medium/heavy REEs (Sm, Gd, Tb, Dy, Lu, Sc, Y) plus related magnets, in response to US tariffs. Within weeks, US, European and Japanese automakers reported disruptions, and a 90-day truce followed. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earths_trade_dispute)
- **9 Oct 2025:** Expanded controls covering five more elements (Ho, Er, Tm, Eu, Yb), a "strict foreign direct product rule" (licence required if China-origin REE is ≥0.1% of value), and an embargo on outflow of skilled Chinese nationals and proprietary technology to foreign projects. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later); [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- **30 Oct 2025, Busan:** Trump and Xi agreed that China would suspend the October expansion for one year. Trump cut tariffs on Chinese goods from 57% to 47% and said, "All of the rare earth has been settled." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earths_trade_dispute); [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later). The suspension runs to 10 Nov 2026 under MOFCOM Announcement No. 70. — [technology.org, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/19/china-rare-earth-export-pause-november-10/). The IEA likewise lists further REE restrictions and the proposed equipment/technology controls as "suspended until Nov 2026". — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- **Dec 2025, "general licences":** China began issuing year-long general licences to large magnet makers (JL Mag, Ningbo Yunsheng, Beijing Zhong Ke San Huan) covering exports to individual customers (Reuters, via secondary). Applications tied to defense or sensitive technology still face tougher scrutiny. Capital Economics told the FT that Beijing appeared to be testing the truce by slowing approvals. — [Superhero deep dive](https://www.superhero.com.au/news-insights/rare-earths-and-the-truce-that-moved-a-date-the-deep-dive/); [Rare Earth Exchanges on EU licences](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/china-eu-rare-earth-export-licenses-dfars/) (secondary)
- **6 Jan 2026:** MOFCOM Announcement No. 1 of 2026 bans all dual-use exports to Japanese military end users and end uses after PM Takaichi's Taiwan remarks. The ban extends to intermediaries producing for Japan's military. — [CNBC, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/07/china-bans-dual-use-goods-exports-for-japan-military-over-taiwan-remarks.html); [Defense News, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/01/07/japan-decries-chinese-export-restrictions-that-could-cripple-defenses/). Japan's rare earth and gallium supplies were reportedly cut "to near zero" in 2026. — [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon). CSIS says China "leveraged rare earth export restrictions against Japan in 2026 to deter support for Taiwan." — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **May 2026:** Two Japanese employees of a major Japanese company were detained in Dalian on suspected smuggling of export-restricted rare-earth items. **18 Jun 2026:** Shanghai Customs took compulsory measures against an optics firm's chairman for disguising germanium lenses, after reviewing about three years of records. — [Morgan Lewis, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/recent-china-export-control-actions-signal-active-enforcement-for-rare-earths-and-strategic-minerals)
- **22 Jun 2026:** MOFCOM added 10 US entities, including **MP Materials and USA Rare Earth**, to its export control list. Parties anywhere are barred from supplying them China-origin dual-use items. China's Ministry of Finance also restricted government procurement from 46 US firms, in retaliation for the Pentagon's 1260H list update. — [Morgan Lewis, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/recent-china-export-control-actions-signal-active-enforcement-for-rare-earths-and-strategic-minerals); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earths_trade_dispute)
- **24 Jun 2026:** MOFCOM Announcement No. 26 of 2026 set up a public reporting (whistleblower) mechanism for strategic-mineral export violations, effective 1 Jul 2026. It covers unlicensed exports, disguising items by disassembly, third-country routing, and unauthorised acceptance of foreign-government verification requests. — [Morgan Lewis, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/recent-china-export-control-actions-signal-active-enforcement-for-rare-earths-and-strategic-minerals)
- **Sep 2026 summit and truce extension (conflicting reports):** The Trump–Xi summit in late September is dated 24 Sep by [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm) and 25 Sep by [Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/news/china-rare-earth-magnet-exports-september-2026/). Treasury Secretary Bessent announced on 23 Sep that the truce was extended by only two months, to **10 Jan 2027**. MOFCOM reportedly confirmed on 28 Sep that the "Kuala Lumpur joint arrangement" runs to that date, but its statement does not name rare earths. MOFCOM also said it "believes a positive solution on a further extension can be found" before year-end. — [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/us-and-china-extend-their-rare-earth-truce-by-just-two-months-to-january/); [RAWMATERIALS.net](https://rawmaterials.net/rare-earth-export-controls-china-confirms-extension-of-trade-arrangements-with-u-s/); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/chinas-rare-earth-truce-puts-3-stocks-on-a-jan-10-clock). **Contradicted by** [technology.org, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/19/china-rare-earth-export-pause-november-10/), which says Beijing had *not* published a notice extending Announcement No. 70 beyond 10 Nov 2026. [VOA, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.voanews.com/a/us-races-to-build-rare-earth-supply-chain-as-china-tightens-controls-20261014/8211313.html) also says the summit "extended the trade truce by only two months", with rare earths "a key unresolved issue".
- Escalation options China has not yet used include controls on five more elements plus silicon metal, manganese and magnesium. — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)

**Effects on US companies**
- US imports never recovered to 2024 levels. In Nov 2025, total Chinese magnet exports rose 13%, shipments to Europe jumped 60% year on year, and US imports *fell 11%*. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- Yttrium: China shipped just 17 t to the US in Apr–Dec 2025, versus 333 t in the prior eight months. It shipped 20 t in Feb 2026, against more than 66 t in Jan 2025. Aerospace makers (thermal barrier coatings) reported rationing and possible production pauses. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- Chinese magnet shipments to the US totalled 3,521 t in Jan–Jul 2026 (about 503 t/month), versus 4,212 t in Jan–Jul 2024 (about 601 t/month). They were 647 t in Jul 2026 and 512 t in Aug 2026 (down 21% m/m and 13% y/y). — [Discovery Alert, 22 Sep 2026, citing Chinese customs](https://discoveryalert.com/news/china-rare-earth-magnet-exports-september-2026/)
- Defense-related licence applications were "systematically denied" while overall global volumes held roughly steady. End-use disclosures in applications reportedly feed Beijing's intelligence gathering. — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm); [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon)
- Bloomberg reportedly said in July 2026 that "major contractors and subcontracting partners" struggled to deliver defense platforms to the Pentagon on time because of magnet shortages from China. — [search-result summary of rareearthexchanges/Bloomberg (secondary)](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/chinas-november-rare-earth-magnet-cliff-america-has-92-days-to-prepare-for-a-risk-it-cannot-yet-engineer-away/)
- CSIS: "even if China continues to suspend its export restrictions going into 2027, it is not a reliable export partner to the United States during times of heightened geopolitical tensions." — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- USTR Greer said China's management of rare earth exports creates ongoing uncertainty. — [Discovery Alert, 22 Sep 2026](https://discoveryalert.com/news/china-rare-earth-magnet-exports-september-2026/)

### Inferences
- The toolkit now has five layers: (1) element and product licensing (Apr 2025, permanent); (2) extraterritorial FDPR and technology/personnel bans (Oct 2025, suspended and usable as a bargaining chip); (3) entity-specific denial targeting US ex-China champions such as MP Materials and USA Rare Earth (Jun 2026); (4) end-user/military bans, piloted on Japan (Jan 2026); (5) enforcement and intelligence (Announcement No. 26, customs prosecutions, end-use data mining). Beijing can tighten each layer separately without a headline "ban".
- The short two-month truce extensions (to 10 Jan 2027) and the near-coincidence with the DFARS 1 Jan 2027 date create a "double cliff" in Nov 2026 to Jan 2027.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the Dec 2024 MOFCOM ban on gallium/germanium/antimony exports to the US, or its reported Nov 2025 suspension, because the search budget was exhausted. The report writer should treat those details as needing confirmation.
- I found no reliable detail on a Chinese "tracking system" for magnets (for example, serial-number or end-use tracing) beyond the licence end-use disclosures and Announcement No. 26.
- Licence approval rates (percentage approved) were not found.

## 3. Price manipulation, flooding capacity and two-tier (China vs ex-China) pricing

### Takeaway
China's state-directed overproduction has repeatedly bankrupted or crippled Western rivals (Molycorp, 2015). Since 2025 the market has split: Chinese domestic prices stay low, while ex-China heavy REE prices run about 4–5 times higher. Western projects therefore depend on government price floors ($110/kg NdPr) rather than market prices.

### Cited Findings
- After China's price cuts, Molycorp went bankrupt in June 2015. Mountain Pass was sold out of bankruptcy in June 2017, with China's Shenghe gaining exclusive marketing rights to its output. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earths_trade_dispute)
- "State-directed Chinese overproduction and stockpiling can flood global markets." — [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon)
- The DoD price floor exists to shield MP "from depressed prices driven by Chinese overproduction". — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- IEA: the global market is small, so "even a limited number of new entrants could materially affect supply-demand balances, increasing the risk of oversupply and price downturns", which raises the cost of capital. — [IEA REE report (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)
- European prices for Dy and Tb are "around five times higher than Chinese domestic prices" (early 2026). — [IEA GCMO 2026, executive summary](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- 2026 indicative prices (trade press, not transaction-verified): China NdPr about $109–110/kg; ex-China NdPr about $120–140/kg; China Dy oxide about $205–227/kg vs Rotterdam about $1,074/kg; ex-China Dy quotes ranging from $575 to over $2,500/kg. The SMM NdPr benchmark fell from $126.16/kg (1 Apr 2026) to about $99.61/kg (early May 2026), then recovered. — [Rare Earth Exchanges (Dy/Tb divide)](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/dysprosium-terbium-china-ex-china-price-divide/); [Mainrich, Jun 2026](https://www.mainrichinternational.com/resources/articles/rare-earth-market-update-june-2026-ndpr-mofcom-western-supply); [Rare Earth Exchanges, 2026 gap](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/heavy-rare-earth-china-ex-china-price-gap/)
- Floors in place: DoD–MP 10-year NdPr floor of $110/kg; US–Lynas four-year deal (about $96M of purchases) at a $110/kg NdPr floor; Japan–Lynas 12-year deal at the same floor; Serra Verde Dy floor of about $575/kg. — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.178](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf); [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/dysprosium-terbium-china-ex-china-price-divide/)
- Rare earths make up about 40% of permanent-magnet cost but under 1% of vehicle value, so end users can afford to pay a premium. — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- A US–Japan action plan (Feb 2026) works toward "border-adjusted price floors". — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- Rare-earth prices rose about 23% while miners' shares fell about 28%, a "disconnect" (headline). — [ZeroHedge (headline only, low-quality source)](https://www.zerohedge.com/commodities/great-rare-earth-disconnect-prices-23-miners-down-28)

### Inferences
- The two-tier market both helps and hurts the US. It lets ex-China producers capture premiums, but it signals that China can collapse the premium at will by opening the licence tap, as happened in 2012–2015. Long-term floors and offtakes (DoD, Japan) are therefore the main protection against predatory pricing, and they cover only a fraction of supply.

### Gaps
- I found no Benchmark, Adamas or SMM primary price series. Ex-China prices are thinly traded and opaque.

## 4. Specific obstacles facing the US

### Takeaway
Mining is not the binding constraint; separation (especially HREE), metallisation, magnet equipment and know-how are. The other obstacles are an operating-cost gap of 2.5 to 10 times China's, very long US permitting and lead times, radioactive and chemical waste handling, light-REE-skewed US geology, scarce engineers, small volatile markets that deter private capital, and a history of policy that surges in crises and fades afterwards.

### Cited Findings
- **Cost gap:** Crack-leach operating costs outside China are "2.5 times to 10 times more expensive than in China", because of stricter environmental controls, costlier chemicals, energy and radioactive by-product management. Hard-rock deposits (typical in the US) have "nearly double the capital intensity and triple the operating costs of ionic clays". Reagents are 15–25% of conversion costs and are cheaper in China. — [IEA REE report (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf); [IEA GCMO 2026, p.287](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- **Permitting and lead times:** Baskaran says it takes an average of **29 years** to build a mine in the US. — [MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/chinas-rare-earth-clampdown-backfired-csis-analyst/). New REE mines outside China and Myanmar average about **8 years** of lead time. — [IEA REE report (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf). Diversified regions face "more complex permitting requirements and fragmented supply chains". — [IEA REE report](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)
- **HREE complexity and geology:** HREE separation can require "up to 40 times more stages than for light rare earth separation". — [IEA REE report](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf). Baskaran cites "upward of 50 stages" and says US deposits are dominated by light REE, so the US must secure HREE through partnerships. — [MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/chinas-rare-earth-clampdown-backfired-csis-analyst/). There are no proven processes to extract HREE from ionic clays without the acid leaching used in China and Myanmar. — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.287](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- **Environmental and process chemistry:** China uses chloride (HCl) routes with PVC tanks. Most ex-China suppliers use nitric routes, which need costlier stainless-steel cells and "very strong wastewater management systems", and "there is limited experience in tackling this issue systematically outside China and Japan". — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.287](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf). Round Top (USA Rare Earth) heap leaching is "highly reagent intensive" and raises waste management and environmental handling challenges. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **Equipment:** Alloy, metallisation and magnet equipment outside China is "5-12 times more expensive" with "two to three times longer lead times". Alignment presses are about 10 times costlier. Grain-boundary-diffusion (GBD) equipment has a single non-China supplier at about 12 times the cost, is subject to Chinese tech-export controls, and is heavily patented and litigated. Energy-efficient molten-salt electrolysis cell designs "remain closely held and [are] not widely available outside China". Strip casters have few suppliers outside China. — [IEA REE report (2026)](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf)
- **Know-how:** There is a "considerable lack of equilibrium data" for solvent extraction and "major empirical and heuristic knowledge gaps" in sintering and high-precision cutting. — [IEA REE report](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf). Rare Earth Exchanges describes the gap as "manufacturing memory — powder metallurgy, coercivity control". — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- **Unproven technologies:** Chromatography is "generally not considered commercially scalable" for primary, high-volume separation. Many novel methods are unproven at scale. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **Workforce:** The US graduates only "200 to 250 mining engineers" a year (David Copley, State Dept.). — [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon); [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm). China's Oct 2025 package also embargoed outflows of skilled Chinese nationals to foreign projects. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **Capital and price risk:** The IEA estimates a further **~$60bn** of rare earth investment is needed outside China by 2035 to close gaps. Small market size, long ramp-ups and long paybacks raise the cost of capital. — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.260](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf); [IEA REE report](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/88d2b060-4c5b-46cc-8727-c60576cb937d/RareearthelementsPathwaystosecureanddiversifiedsupplychains.pdf). CSIS says success requires "flows of private capital that are buoyed by, but not reliant on, positive government signals". — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **Offtake and willingness to pay:** Even if new projects come online, "their sustained operations will depend on consumer motivation to buy materials from diversified suppliers." — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.182](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- **Fragmentation and single points of failure:** Four of the five highest-risk minerals on the 2025 US Critical Minerals List are rare earths. Single points of failure remain, "especially for heavy rare earth feedstock". — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- **Midstream/downstream taper:** Announced ex-China projects reach close to 50 kt REE of mining capacity by 2035, under 40 kt of refining, and only about 18 kt REE (about 57 kt NdFeB) of metal/alloy/magnet capacity. — [IEA GCMO 2026, pp.178, 182](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- **Policy volatility:** The US should avoid "the historical pattern of ramping up during crises only to scale back in periods of calm." — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later). DFARS 252.225-7052 was already delayed one year from 2026, and contractors are lobbying for a second delay (FT via Adamas). — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- **Allied politics:** Europeans worry that US investments in European rare-earth firms could become leverage on other disputes, and there is no joint economic-deterrence or compensation mechanism. — [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon)
- **Chinese retaliation against US champions:** MP Materials and USA Rare Earth were placed on China's export control list (22 Jun 2026). — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/recent-china-export-control-actions-signal-active-enforcement-for-rare-earths-and-strategic-minerals)

**Policy response so far (context for obstacles)**
- The US government has committed more than $7.3bn across five agencies (CSIS, Apr 2026). VOA, citing CSIS data, puts non-equity investment at $7.6bn from 2025 through June 2026, more than four times the 2020–24 total. Instruments include $400M of DoD equity in MP, a $150M DoD loan for MP HREE separation, a $620M loan to Vulcan Elements, a $1.6bn Commerce CHIPS letter of intent for USA Rare Earth, about $4bn of EXIM letters of intent, $2bn more for the National Defense Stockpile, Project Vault, a 49% DoD stake in a Maaden refinery in Saudi Arabia, and $565M of DFC support for Serra Verde. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later); [VOA, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.voanews.com/a/us-races-to-build-rare-earth-supply-chain-as-china-tightens-controls-20261014/8211313.html)
- G7 leaders (June 2026) set a target of reducing dependence on any single non-G7 supplier of rare earths and permanent magnets to **below 60% by 2030**. — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- Obstacles ranked by how long each takes to fix (my synthesis): (1) HREE feedstock and separation, the slowest, which depends on geology, foreign partners and a 40–50-stage process; (2) magnet-making equipment and process know-how; (3) metallisation (electrolysis cell design); (4) permitting and workforce; (5) cost and price risk, which can be offset quickly with money (floors, offtakes) but needs that money sustained politically for 10 or more years.

### Gaps
- I found no quantified NEPA permitting durations specific to REE projects in 2026 sources, beyond Baskaran's 29-year mine average.
- I did not find a recent RAND or Brookings obstacles study.

## 5. Expert and institutional timeline estimates (with dates)

### Takeaway
Estimates cluster as follows:
- Defense-grade light-REE magnets at meaningful scale: late 2020s, with "a considerable portion of US defense needs by end of decade" per CSIS.
- Commercial-scale light REE: end of decade into the early 2030s.
- Heavy REE: mid-2030s, with Project Blue the more optimistic outlier at "end of decade".

## 6. Segment-by-segment timeline with scenarios (my synthesis, anchored to cited data)

### Takeaway
My synthesis, built on the sources above: the US has effectively reached independence in light-REE mining, could reach defense-scale independence in LREE separation and magnets around 2028–2030, and is unlikely to be independent in HREE separation, metals and GBD equipment before the mid-2030s, and later than that for commercial (EV/wind) volumes.

### Cited Findings (anchors used)
- US production of REE compounds and metals at Mountain Pass reached 8,900 t in 2025, its highest in decades, but covered only one-third of consumption. The remaining 18,100 t was imported: 71% from China, 13% Malaysia, 5% Japan, 5% Estonia. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- US magnet capacity is on track for about 5,000 t by end-2026, against about 48,000 t of demand (secondary). — [OilPrice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/America-Needs-48000-Tons-of-Rare-Earth-Magnets-It-Only-Makes-300.html) (headline: "America Needs 48,000 Tons of Rare Earth Magnets. - Planned ex-China metal/alloy/magnet output is about 57 kt NdFeB by 2035, "a modest pipeline led by the United States". — [IEA GCMO 2026](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)

## 7. Cut-off scenario: what if China stops supplying the US in 2026–2027?

### Takeaway
A full Chinese cut-off in 2026–27 would leave the US short of most of its imported REE compounds and metals and most of its magnets. The HREE shortfall would be near-total. The IEA puts the economic value at risk for the US and Europe combined at about $3.5tn/yr. Defense programs would hit stockpile limits within months.

### Cited Findings
- If China's controls were "implemented in full, the economic value of downstream production at risk would reach USD 6.5 trillion per year for countries outside China", about 7% of their combined GDP. The US and Europe face the largest exposure. In the US, autos account for more than 40% of losses, and data-centre servers and defense for 15% each. — [IEA GCMO 2026, p.238](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/19c60aa0-ee07-4a20-a138-33c967ac5008/GlobalCriticalMineralsOutlook2026.pdf)
- A complete Chinese block of permanent-magnet supply would cost the US and Europe about $3.5tn/yr, excluding second-order effects (IEA, via CFR). — [CFR/Teer, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon)
- Of US imports of REE compounds and metals (18,100 t in 2025), 71% came from China. — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- Chinese magnet exports to the US ran at about 500 t/month in Jan–Jul 2026 (3,521 t). — [Discovery Alert, 22 Sep 2026](https://discoveryalert.com/news/china-rare-earth-magnet-exports-september-2026/)
- US HREE import reliance is 100%. Defense demand is in the thousands of tons per year, while Lynas made 8 t of Dy+Tb in Q1 2026. — [Tech Times, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- About 60 days of military REE supply on hand (SCMP via a Quest Metals blog; secondhand, older). — [Quest Metals](https://www.questmetals.com/blog/scmp-us-has-two-months-of-rare-earth-supplies-left). An older analysis says HREE stocks of "only hundreds of kilograms" would "vanish within months". — [Rare Earth Exchanges](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/u-s-heavy-rare-earth-stockpiles-under-strain-amid-chinas-export-curbs/)
- China is "highly likely to cut off rare earth exports to the United States and its allies in the event of escalating conflict in the Indo-Pacific". — [CSIS, Apr 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)
- One analysis argues the November 2026 risk is selective licensing rather than a midnight shut-off. — [Rare Earth Exchanges, "November Rare-Earth Magnet Cliff"](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/chinas-november-rare-earth-magnet-cliff-america-has-92-days-to-prepare-for-a-risk-it-cannot-yet-engineer-away/)
- The US is building a 60-day emergency mineral reserve (Feb 2026 initiative, modelled on the Strategic Petroleum Reserve) through Project Vault. The National Defense Stockpile received an additional $2bn. — [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/us-races-to-build-rare-earth-supply-chain-as-china-tightens-controls-20261014/8211313.html); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/rare-earth-export-restrictions-one-year-later)

### Inferences (author's rough arithmetic; not an institutional estimate)
- **REE compounds and metals:** 71% of 18,100 t is about 12,900 t/yr lost, or about half of 2025 US consumption of about 27,000 t (8,900 domestic plus 18,100 imported). Malaysia, Japan and Estonia supplies (about 5,200 t) continue, but some of them depend on Chinese intermediates or are subject to the 0.1% extraterritorial rule if it is reinstated.
- **Magnets:** A loss of about 6,000 t/yr of direct Chinese magnet imports (500 t/month), plus magnets embedded in imported subassemblies, which are probably larger and unquantified. Against about 5,000 t of US capacity by end-2026 (secondary), the US would cover perhaps 10–15% of total magnet demand. That would be enough to prioritise most defense needs but not automotive. Domestic magnets would also still need HREE (Dy/Tb) for high-temperature grades, which only Lynas and early MP/Energy Fuels circuits could supply in tonnes, not hundreds of tonnes.
- **HREE:** A near-total shortfall (about 90–100%) for Dy, Tb, Y, Sm, Lu and Gd in 2026–27. The yttrium episode (shipments down about 95%) shows aerospace would ration within months. Samarium shortages would threaten SmCo defense magnets.
- **Sector impact:** Auto production halts within weeks to months (as in May–June 2025). Defense primes slip deliveries. The 2027 DFARS deadline would almost certainly be waived or delayed.
- **If the 0.1% de minimis FDPR were reinstated after 10 Nov 2026 or 10 Jan 2027** (as opposed to a total cut-off), allied magnets made with any Chinese REE input would also need Chinese licences. That would extend the shortfall to Japanese, Korean and European supply and erode the "friend-shoring" buffer.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous government or think-tank model of US stockpile depletion months under a total cut-off. The 60-day figure is secondhand and dated.
- I found no quantification of magnets embedded in imported components (motors, actuators, electronics), which is likely the largest hidden exposure.
