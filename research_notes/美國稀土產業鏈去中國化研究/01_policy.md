## 1. Executive actions: EO 14241, DPA, Section 232, NEDC, FAST-41, and what happened in 2026

### Takeaway
The administration's main tools have been executive: EO 14241 (Mar 2025) handed DPA Title III and financing authority to DoD and DFC and fast-tracked permitting. The Section 232 investigation into processed critical minerals ended on 14 Jan 2026 **without tariffs**. It opened a negotiation track built on price floors and minimum import prices instead. The other big 2026 actions are Project Vault (a ~$12B EXIM-backed reserve, announced Feb 2026, first traders signed 23 Sep 2026) and allied "price-floor" trade frameworks. As of this writing, no Section 232 tariff or minimum import price on rare earths/magnets has been confirmed.

### Cited Findings
**EO 14241 "Immediate Measures to Increase American Mineral Production" (signed 20 Mar 2025; 90 Fed. Reg. 13673, 25 Mar 2025)**
- Sec. 3: within 10 days agencies list pending mineral project applications for the National Energy Dominance Council (NEDC) Chair. Within 15 days the NEDC Chair submits projects to the Permitting Dashboard as **FAST-41 transparency projects**, and the Permitting Council publishes expedited schedules. — [White House EO 14241](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/immediate-measures-to-increase-american-mineral-production/)
- Sec. 4: NEDC to recommend clarifications to the Mining Act of 1872 on waste rock and tailings within 30 days. — [White House EO 14241](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/immediate-measures-to-increase-american-mineral-production/)
- Sec. 5: Interior lists federal lands with known mineral deposits and makes mineral production "the primary land use" there (10 days). DoD, Interior, USDA and DOE identify sites for commercial mineral production leasing, with extended-use leases on DoD/DOE land. — [White House EO 14241](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/immediate-measures-to-increase-american-mineral-production/)
- Sec. 6(b): **waives DPA §303 requirements (50 U.S.C. 4533(a)(1)–(a)(6)) and delegates §303 authority to the Secretary of Defense.** Adds mineral production as a priority area of DoD's Industrial Base Analysis and Sustainment (IBAS) program. — [White House EO 14241](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/immediate-measures-to-increase-american-mineral-production/)
- Sec. 6(d)–(e): gives the DFC CEO DPA §§301–303 authority for domestic mineral production loans, guarantees and political risk insurance. Orders a DFC/DoD plan for a dedicated mineral production investment fund within 30 days. — [White House EO 14241](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/immediate-measures-to-increase-american-mineral-production/)
- Sec. 6(f): EXIM to issue guidance for the Supply Chain Resiliency Initiative and the Make More in America Initiative within 30 days. — [White House EO 14241](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/immediate-measures-to-increase-american-mineral-production/)
- Sec. 6(c): agencies with loan, grant, equity or offtake authority must "take steps to rescind" policies that require applicants to submit **SEC Regulation S-K 1300** mining disclosures. — [White House EO 14241](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/immediate-measures-to-increase-american-mineral-production/); House Democrats call this a waiver of DPA congressional-notification requirements, investment limits and S-K 1300 that "increases financial risk to taxpayers" — [House Natural Resources Dems letter, 2 Feb 2026](https://democrats-naturalresources.house.gov/imo/media/doc/2026-02-02_moc_to_defense_commerce_energy_interior_re_mineral_equity_deals_oversight.pdf)
- A June 2025 presidential directive called for a common application system for federal funding of critical mineral projects. — [Covington, Inside Government Contracts, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.insidegovernmentcontracts.com/2026/02/federal-push-for-critical-minerals-stockpiling-2025-in-review-and-outlook-for-2026/)

**Section 232 investigation into processed critical minerals and derivative products (PCMDPs)**
- Initiated 23 Apr 2025 under EO 14272. Commerce findings went to the President on 24 Oct 2025. — [Covington, Jan 2026](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/trump-administration-announces-results-of-critical-minerals-investigation-under-section-232-and-directs-us-officials-to-initiate-negotiations)
- Scope widened on 7 Nov 2025 to add boron, copper, lead, metallurgical coal, phosphate, potash, rhenium, silicon, silver and uranium. — [ST&R / web summary](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/tariff-actions-resources/section-232-investigation-critical-minerals)
- **Proclamation 11001**, "Adjusting Imports of Processed Critical Minerals and Their Derivative Products into the United States". The White House page dates it **14 Jan 2026**; Covington says 15 Jan 2026. The difference is likely signing date vs. issuance date. — [White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/adjusting-imports-of-processed-critical-minerals-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states/); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/trump-administration-announces-results-of-critical-minerals-investigation-under-section-232-and-directs-us-officials-to-initiate-negotiations)
  - Findings: PCMDP imports threaten to impair national security. As of 2024 the US was **100% net-import reliant for 12 critical minerals and ≥50% reliant for 29 more**. The US is the **second-largest producer of mined rare earth oxides**, but limited processing means oxides are exported for refining and then re-imported. Domestic rare earth permanent magnet output meets "only a fraction of defense needs." — [White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/adjusting-imports-of-processed-critical-minerals-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states/)
  - Remedy: **no tariffs.** Commerce and USTR are to negotiate agreements and "consider price floors for trade in critical minerals and other trade-restricting measures." The President may later impose "minimum import prices for specific types of critical minerals." An update was due within 180 days (~13 Jul 2026). Legal authority: 19 U.S.C. 1862. — [White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/adjusting-imports-of-processed-critical-minerals-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states/)
  - Law firms called it "No Section 232 Tariff Now." — [Thompson Hine, Jan 2026](https://www.thompsonhinesmartrade.com/2026/01/no-section-232-tariff-now-on-processed-critical-minerals-and-their-derivative-products/)
- Related precedent: in the **polysilicon** Section 232 action (6 Aug 2026), the President imposed a 15% tariff plus **minimum import prices** enforced through specific tariffs, effective 4 Dec 2026. The same MIP template could be used for rare earths. — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/president-trump-orders-tariffs-and-price-floors-polysilicon-section-232-action)
- 4 Feb 2026: the administration proposed a preferential trading zone for allies with enforceable fair-market prices at each production stage. The USTR comment deadline on border-adjustable price floors was 19 Mar 2026. The administration reportedly plans to use Section 232 tariffs to enforce a border-adjusted price floor. — [Mayer Brown, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/critical-minerals-project-vault-and-the-new-us-critical-minerals-playbook)
- 4 Feb 2026: USTR announced critical minerals cooperation with the European Commission and Japan, with action plans meant to "lay the groundwork for a binding plurilateral agreement." A G7 finance ministers' proposal for rare earth price floors (12 Jan 2026) was still at an early stage. — [Foley, Jan 2026](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/01/president-trump-issues-section-232-proclamation-to-establish-a-reliable-domestic/); [Morgan Lewis, Feb 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/02/section-232-investigations-prompt-trade-negotiation)

**Critical Minerals Ministerial and bilateral frameworks**
- Feb 2026 Critical Minerals Ministerial, hosted by Sec. Rubio: frameworks announced with Argentina, Cook Islands, Ecuador, Guinea, Morocco, Paraguay, Peru, Philippines, UAE, UK and Uzbekistan. Substance not disclosed; talks reportedly underway with 17 more countries. The Oct 2025 Australia and Japan frameworks include commitments to cooperate on price-floor mechanisms. — [Mayer Brown, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/critical-minerals-project-vault-and-the-new-us-critical-minerals-playbook); [State Dept](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2026/02/2026-critical-minerals-ministerial)
- A US–EU–Canada trilateral effort was cited at about $6.4B of combined capital for supply chain resilience (joint, not US-only). — [TechTimes, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)

**Project Vault (US Strategic Critical Minerals Reserve)**
- Announced by President Trump at a White House event in **Feb 2026** (Bloomberg reported a $12B stockpile on 2 Feb 2026). Funding: **up to $10B EXIM direct loan**, the largest commitment in EXIM's 92-year history, plus about **$2B private capital**, for **~$12B** in total. — [Mayer Brown, 12 Mar 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/critical-minerals-project-vault-and-the-new-us-critical-minerals-playbook); [Covington, 4 Feb 2026](https://www.insidegovernmentcontracts.com/2026/02/federal-push-for-critical-minerals-stockpiling-2025-in-review-and-outlook-for-2026/)
- Scope: all 60 minerals on the USGS 2025 list, with initial emphasis on rare earths. Named procurement agents: Hartree Partners, Traxys North America, Mercuria. US OEM participants make long-term commitments and pay a commitment fee, storage and interest. They may withdraw stored material annually if they replenish it. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/critical-minerals-project-vault-and-the-new-us-critical-minerals-playbook)
- Governance: VaultCo is an independently governed private company. EXIM is financier with a non-controlling board observer seat. Drawdown decisions rest with manufacturers, not federal officials. Executive chairman: Brett Lambert. **Executed step: on 23 Sep 2026 Glencore and Mercuria became the first two participants, committing $1B combined.** CSIS's article does not specifically mention rare earths. — [CSIS, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.csis.org/analysis/project-vault-moves-concept-execution)
- Secondary sources say each received a $500M EXIM facility, and that the program lacks explicit congressional authorization. — [search summaries incl. Discovery Alert / BPC](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/article/project-vault-key-issues-for-congress/) (BPC page could not be fetched directly; treat as secondary.)

**Equity-for-grants model, summarized**
- In 2025, federal grants were converted into equity stakes in seven mining and processing companies. The administration reportedly considered an ~8% stake in Critical Minerals Corp. (Tanbreez rare earths, Greenland). — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/critical-minerals-project-vault-and-the-new-us-critical-minerals-playbook)

### Inferences
- The administration prefers **price-based tools** (MP's $110/kg floor, take-or-pay offtakes, allied price floors, MIPs) over tariffs for rare earths. A tariff on Chinese magnets would raise US OEM costs while domestic capacity is still under ~10 kt/yr. Expect any Section 232 action on rare earths to look like the polysilicon MIP template rather than a flat tariff.
- Project Vault is the first civilian-industry stockpile, but as of Oct 2026 only $1B of the $12B is committed by traders. How much is rare-earth-specific is not disclosed.

### Gaps
- Contents of the ~13 Jul 2026 Section 232 negotiation update, and whether any minimum import price or tariff on rare earths/magnets has been proclaimed since. No source found; search budget ran out.
- FAST-41 transparency project list: names and count of the mining projects added since Mar 2025, especially rare earth projects such as Round Top, Halleck Creek or La Paz. Not retrieved. TechTimes notes South32 Hermosa finished FAST-41 permitting in ~3 years vs a 7–10 year industry average ([TechTimes, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328654/20261006/congress-moves-break-china-rare-earth-grip-before-november-deadline.htm)).
- An "Executive Order 14415" restricting DFARS magnet waivers from Jan 2027 was mentioned by one trade-blog search summary but **could not be verified**. Do not use without checking the Federal Register.

## 2. Department of Defense / "Department of War": equity stakes, OSC loans, DPA Title III, price floors, offtakes, NDS

### Takeaway
DoD/DoW has become the anchor investor in US rare earths. The anchor deal is **MP Materials (Jul 2025)**: $400M preferred stock (~15% as-converted), a 10-year $110/kg NdPr floor, 100% offtake from the 10X magnet plant, and a $150M heavy rare earth loan. The floor has **actually paid cash**: about $59.8M in 1H 2026. Follow-on deals: **Vulcan Elements/ReElement** (Nov 2025, $1.4B package incl. $620M + $80M OSC loans), **USA Rare Earth** (Jan 2026 CHIPS LOI for $1.6B; Aug 2026 DoW $750M investment in a Serra Verde offtake SPV), plus equity in Lithium Americas, Trilogy Metals and Korea Zinc. The OBBBA (Jul 2025) funded this with ~$7.5B of DoD critical minerals money.

### Cited Findings
**MP Materials: DoD transaction (effective 9 Jul 2025, announced 10 Jul, closed 11 Jul 2025). Status: executed.**
- Equity: **400,000 shares of Series A Preferred at $1,000 stated value = $400M**. 7.0% PIK dividend; conversion price $30.03. Up to $350M of additional Series A commitments. — [MP Materials 8-K, Jul 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312525157310/d43796d8k.htm)
- Warrant for **11,201,659 shares at $30.03**, 10-year term. Preferred plus warrant = **15% of common** at closing, capped at 19.9% beneficial ownership. — [MP 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312525157310/d43796d8k.htm)
- **Price Protection Agreement: $110/kg NdPr floor for 10 years** from the first full quarter after closing. DoD pays the quarterly shortfall versus the benchmark VWAP, covering concentrate, oxide and metal. Once 10X reaches full capacity, DoD gets 30% of the NdPr price above $110/kg. — [MP 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312525157310/d43796d8k.htm)
- **10X Facility offtake:** DoD buys 100% of 10X magnet output for 10 years from the commercial operation date, at the project company's production cost, with possible syndication to commercial buyers. Threshold EBITDA is $140M/yr, inflation-adjusted at 2%. DoD pays quarterly "magnet facilitation payments" equal to 25% of the Threshold EBITDA. DoD takes the first $30M of EBITDA above the threshold, then 50% of the rest. DoD covers certain incremental costs up to $30M/yr. — [MP 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312525157310/d43796d8k.htm)
- **$150M DoD Samarium Project Loan** (unsecured, 12 years, 10-year UST + 1.00%) for heavy rare earth separation incl. samarium at Mountain Pass. Private lenders JPMorgan and Goldman committed ≥$1B, with $650M for 10X. Independence (TX) magnet plant to expand to 3,000 t/yr. — [MP 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1801368/000119312525157310/d43796d8k.htm)
- **Executed cash flows in 2026:** PPA income was $17.58M in Q2 2026 and **$59.8M in 1H 2026**, implying ~$42.2M in Q1. Q1 is confirmed at $42.3M from the 10-Q. 1H 2026 revenue was $199M (+68% y/y); NdPr output 1,757 t (+51%); concentrate sales to China stopped Jul 2025. — [Zacks via Yahoo Finance, 14 Aug 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/mp-sees-68-surge-revenues-163500550.html); [StockTitan 10-Q summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/MP/10-q-mp-materials-corp-de-quarterly-earnings-report-0aa26d444b01.html). One profile's figure of "$132.9M combined PPA income" does not reconcile with these and is treated as unverified ([rare-earth-mining.com](https://rare-earth-mining.com/mp-materials/)).
- Status of the build-out: Independence began NdFeB production in Dec 2025, with commercial sales targeted for H2 2026. 10X (Northlake, TX) broke ground Feb 2026, with commissioning expected 2028. Combined US magnet capacity at full build-out is ~10,000 t/yr. — [TechTimes, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)

**Vulcan Elements and ReElement Technologies (announced 3–4 Nov 2025). Status: announced; loan closing not confirmed.**
- A $1.4B partnership with the US government. Vulcan is to build a **10,000 t NdFeB magnet facility**, funded by a **$620M OSC direct loan**, $50M from Commerce (CHIPS), and $550M private capital. ReElement gets an **$80M OSC loan** for refining. DoW receives warrants: joint materials say in both companies, while ReElement's own release names only ReElement. Commerce's $50M is described both as "incentives" and as **equity** in Vulcan. — [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/vulcan-elements-reelement-technologies-sign-1-4b-rare-earth-magnet-supply-deal-with-us/); [GovConWire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/vulcan-reelement-us-rare-earth-magnet-partnership); [Carolina Journal](https://www.carolinajournal.com/durham-rare-earth-start-up-receives-1-4b-federal-investment/)
- OSC loans are funded by the OBBBA, which "provides up to $100 billion in total OSC lending authority" for critical minerals and related industries. — [Pulse2 / company release](https://pulse2.com/vulcan-elements-and-reelement-technologies-launch-1-4-billion-u-s-partnership-to-build-fully-domestic-magnet-supply-chain)

**USA Rare Earth (USAR)**
- **26 Jan 2026: non-binding LOI with Commerce (CHIPS Program)** for a **$1.6B package: $277M federal funding plus a $1.3B senior secured loan**. In return the government gets **16.1M shares and 17.6M warrants**, about 8–16% of USAR per the company (FT: ~10%). Funding is milestone-based, and there is **no price floor**. USAR also raised a $1.5B PIPE anchored by Inflection Point. — [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/us-takes-equity-stake-invests-1-6b-in-rare-earths-miner/); [Engineering News, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/us-to-back-16bn-usa-rare-earth-funding-shares-jump-2026-01-27); [USAR 8-K Jan 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1970622/000121390026007457/ea027403101ex99-1_usarare.htm)
- **24 Aug 2026 (8-K Ex. 99.1):** DoW committed **$750M** to an SPV that buys Serra Verde (Brazil) Phase I output, $250M more than the $500M originally contemplated. The offtake has **take-or-pay and floor prices**. DoW also signed a **forward purchase contract for ≥$300M** of rare earth products over 5 years. The SPV is capitalized at **$1.55B**, including a $500M private bank revolver. The USAR–Serra Verde acquisition was agreed 20 Apr 2026 with a 15-year offtake for 100% of Phase I, including Dy/Tb floor prices. The shareholder vote was 28 Aug 2026. The 8-K does not state whether the DoW $750M is equity or debt, and says Commerce funding "is subject to the achievement of milestones." — [USAR 8-K, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001970622/000121390026092810/ea030292401ex99-1.htm)
- Stillwater, OK magnet plant Phase 1a was commissioned Mar 2026, targeting 600 t/yr. — [TechTimes, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)

### Inferences
- Of the headline packages, the money **actually disbursed** that can be documented is: MP's $400M preferred (closed Jul 2025), the $150M samarium loan (due within 30 days of closing), and ~$60M of floor payments in 1H 2026 (plus Q4 2025, not retrieved). The Vulcan OSC loans, USAR CHIPS money (LOI, milestone-gated) and the USAR SPV $750M are commitments whose drawdown is unconfirmed.
- The floor's cost to DoD falls as NdPr prices rise. Q2 2026 PPA income ($17.6M) was well below Q1 ($42.3M), which implies NdPr prices moved toward $110/kg in Q2. The 30% upside share starts only after 10X reaches full capacity (~2028+).
- DoW is moving from the domestic floor to **foreign offtake with floors** (Serra Verde, Brazil, for Dy/Tb). This acknowledges that domestic heavy rare earth supply cannot meet the Jan 2027 DFARS deadline.

The figures in parentheses come from training knowledge and should not be cited without checking.
- MP Q4 2025 and Q3 2026 PPA payments; whether DoD exercised the extra $350M Series A; 10X capacity (widely reported as 7,000 t; not in the 8-K).
- DPA Title III awards in 2025–26 to Noveon, eVAC Sumter SC, Lynas Texas (Seadrift, whose status was reportedly in doubt), Ucore, and Energy Fuels: not retrieved.
- Whether the Vulcan OSC $620M loan has closed or been disbursed.
- OSC credit subsidy appropriation amount (commonly cited as $500M supporting up to $100B of loans): not verified against statute text.

## 3. DFARS / NDAA sourcing restrictions: the 2027 magnet ban and FY2025–FY2027 NDAA provisions

### Takeaway
From **1 Jan 2027**, DFARS 252.225-7052 (implementing 10 U.S.C. §4872) extends the ban on covered-country (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea) NdFeB and SmCo magnets from "melted or produced" to **"mined, refined, separated, melted, or produced."** This is a mine-to-magnet requirement. The date already reflects a one-year slip. The **FY2027 HASC NDAA Sec. 1801** would replace the 2027 upstream cliff with a tiered, phased framework (25%→100% non-covered sourcing for Tier 2 from 2028–2031; 50% domestic content for Tier 1). It is not law as of Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
- DoD final rule codifying DFARS 252.225-7052 issued **30 May 2024**. Covered materials: SmCo magnets, NdFeB magnets, tantalum metals and alloys, tungsten metal powder, and tungsten heavy alloy. Covered countries: DPRK, PRC, Russia, Iran. — [Z2Data, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.z2data.com/insights/how-the-top-u-s-manufacturers-are-preparing-for-dfars-252-225-7052/)
- Until 1 Jan 2027 the restriction covers material "melted or produced" in covered countries. From 1 Jan 2027 it covers "mined, refined, separated, melted, or produced." — [Z2Data](https://www.z2data.com/insights/how-the-top-u-s-manufacturers-are-preparing-for-dfars-252-225-7052/)
- Statutory chain: the FY2021 NDAA extended the restriction upstream with delayed implementation. **FY2024 NDAA Sec. 854 (P.L. 118-31)** set the 1 Jan 2027 date. — [search summary of NDAA analyses](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/the-pentagons-rare-earth-ultimatum-ban-on-chinese-materials-by-2027/)
- About 78% of Pentagon programs are affected, including the F-35, nuclear submarines and Tomahawk. The date already reflects a one-year delay, and contractors are lobbying for a second delay (single-source claim). — [TechTimes, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- **FY2025 NDAA:** created an additional national security waiver authority for the sensitive-materials restriction. **FY2026 NDAA:** added gallium, germanium and molybdenum to covered materials (Ga/Ge restriction effective **18 Dec 2027**). It also expanded recycling exemptions so material recycled in the US becomes compliant; for non-magnet materials, recycling in reciprocal-defense-agreement countries also qualifies. — [The Fuse / SAFE, 9 Jun 2026](https://thefuse.org/ndaa-defense-critical-minerals-policy-sourcing-restrictions/)
- **FY2027 NDAA, HASC Chairman's Mark, Sec. 1801** (amends 10 U.S.C. §4872):
  - Tier 1 materials keep covered-nation restrictions **plus a 50% domestic cost requirement**. Tier 2 phases in non-covered sourcing: **25% (2028), 50% (2029), 75% (2030), 100% (2031+)**.
  - Strikes the 1 Jan 2027 upstream restriction.
  - DoW reviews tiers at least every 6 months, and materials can move up to Tier 1 but not back.
  - Waivers limited to 2 years with transition plans and binding supplier agreements; "reasonable price" is removed as a waiver basis.
  - Creates a **preferred domestic manufacturers list** and requires annual reports.
  - Source: [The Fuse / SAFE, 9 Jun 2026](https://thefuse.org/ndaa-defense-critical-minerals-policy-sourcing-restrictions/)
- Sec. 1801 covers permanent magnets, tantalum, tungsten, gallium, germanium, molybdenum, niobium and several rare earth oxides and metals. It requires provenance records kept up to 10 years across multi-tier supply chains. **Sec. 1805** creates a Defense Supply Chain Intelligence and Risk Response Program. HASC approved the bill; as of 16 Jul 2026 it had not passed the House and Senate consideration had "hit procedural roadblocks"; "none of these NDAA provisions have become law." — [Rare Earth Exchanges, 16 Jul 2026](https://rareearthexchanges.com/news/ndaa-2027s-quiet-revolution-congress-is-beginning-to-build-the-rules-for-a-post-china-critical-minerals-economy/)
- The mining and permitting bills (H.R. 4090, H.R. 1501, H.R. 5929) are described as candidates for inclusion in the NDAA. — [TechTimes, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328654/20261006/congress-moves-break-china-rare-earth-grip-before-november-deadline.htm)

### Inferences
- With 10 weeks left, the binding question for investors is whether the FY2027 NDAA conference **enacts Sec. 1801 before 1 Jan 2027**. If it does, the upstream cliff becomes a 2028–2031 glide path, a near-term negative for scarcity pricing of compliant heavy rare earths/magnets but a structural positive through domestic-content preference. If it does not, the 2027 rule applies with waiver and nonavailability routes.
- Heavy rare earths (Dy/Tb) are the binding constraint. Lynas produced only ~8 t of Dy/Tb in Q1 2026 ([TechTimes, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)), so nonavailability determinations or waivers are likely in early 2027.

### Gaps
- SASC FY2027 NDAA text, House floor vote and conference status as of Oct 2026: not found.
- Whether DoD has issued a DFARS rule or class deviation in 2026 to implement or delay the 2027 date. None found.
- A claimed "$48.7B for critical minerals" in the FY2027 budget request comes from an investment blog and is unverified.

## 5. Trade: tariffs on Chinese magnets, Section 301, the Busan truce (Oct 2025) and its 2026 status

### Takeaway
**Busan (30 Oct 2025):** China suspended its expanded 9 Oct 2025 rare earth controls for a year and promised general licenses. The US cut the fentanyl tariff by 10 points and suspended the BIS 50% "affiliates rule" and the Section 301 port fees for a year. China's **April 2025 licensing regime on seven medium and heavy rare earths was never lifted**. The **May 2026 Beijing summit** made little rare-earth progress. The **Washington summit (week of 21 Sep 2026)** extended the truce from 10 Nov 2026 to **10 Jan 2027** with rare earths still "under discussion." Separately, a **25% Section 301 tariff on Chinese permanent magnets** was scheduled from 1 Jan 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Busan deal (White House fact sheet, Nov 2025)**
- China: suspends global implementation of the expanded rare earth controls announced **9 Oct 2025**. Issues **general licenses** for rare earths, gallium, germanium, antimony and graphite for US end users and their suppliers worldwide, called "the de facto removal of controls China imposed since 2023." Commits to "effectively eliminate" current and proposed controls. — [White House fact sheet, Nov 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-strikes-deal-on-economic-and-trade-relations-with-china/)

  - Removes 10 points of the fentanyl tariff, effective 10 Nov 2025.
  - Keeps heightened reciprocal tariffs suspended to **10 Nov 2026**, with 10% remaining.
  - Suspends for one year from 10 Nov 2025 the BIS interim final rule "Expansion of End-User Controls to Cover Affiliates of Certain Listed Entities."
  - Suspends the Section 301 maritime/shipbuilding actions for one year.
  - Extends certain Section 301 exclusions to 10 Nov 2026.
  - Source: [White House fact sheet](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-strikes-deal-on-economic-and-trade-relations-with-china/)

- The truce did not deliver its rare-earth promise. The heavy rare earths that matter most for defense (Dy, Tb, Sm, Y) remain licensed, and military end users are excluded. That keeps a large ex-China price premium in place and supports the US floor and offtake model.

## 6. Legislation: 45X after the OBBBA, stockpile and price-support bills, and permitting bills

### Takeaway
The OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, Jul 2025) **ended the permanence of the 45X critical minerals credit**, which now phases down 75%/50%/25% for 2031/2032/2033. It added foreign-entity (PFE) restrictions from 2026 and metallurgical coal as a credit-eligible mineral. It also poured ~$7.5B of DoD money into minerals. In the 119th Congress, House permitting bills have passed, and the bipartisan **SECURE Minerals Act** (Strategic Resilience Reserve, $2.5B Senate version) is pending. No price-support or reserve bill has been enacted.

### Cited Findings
- **45X critical minerals:** the OBBBA removes the no-phase-out exemption. The credit is 75% (2031), 50% (2032), 25% (2033), and ends 31 Dec 2033. Only projects running before 2032 get the full credit, which favors brownfield. — [CSIS, 9 Jul 2025](https://www.csis.org/analysis/impacts-one-big-beautiful-act-mining-sector). Fastmarkets says "eliminated for production after 2034" and S&P says the Senate moved the end to 2034 from the House's 2032. **Sources conflict (2033 vs 2034); check the enacted text.** — [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/critical-minerals-funding-obb/); [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/070125-us-senate-clears-budget-bill-paving-way-to-resume-coal-leases-on-federal-lands)
- **FEOC/PFE rules:** new Specified Foreign Entities and Foreign-Influenced Entities (25%+ ownership, appointment of executives or board members, or contractual control) are barred from 45X from 2026. A "material assistance cost ratio" applies, with Treasury thresholds due by 31 Dec 2027. — [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/impacts-one-big-beautiful-act-mining-sector)

The Senate version, introduced Jan 2026 by Sens. Shaheen (D-NH) and Young (R-IN), proposes **$2.5B**, with a $100M minimum for allied participation. Status: pending. — [TechTimes, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328654/20261006/congress-moves-break-china-rare-earth-grip-before-november-deadline.htm); [Covington](https://www.insidegovernmentcontracts.com/2026/02/federal-push-for-critical-minerals-stockpiling-2025-in-review-and-outlook-for-2026/)

## 8. Assessment: effectiveness, consistency risks, gaps

### Takeaway
US policy has moved from grants to **state capitalism**: equity, price floors, take-or-pay offtake and stockpiles. It has unlocked real private capital, including MP's $1B bank facility and USAR's $1.5B PIPE, and real cash transfers (MP floor payments). Physical de-sinicization lags badly. Planned US magnet capacity is about 10 kt/yr against China's JL MAG alone targeting 60 kt/yr. Heavy rare earth separation is barely starting. The two hard dates are the 1 Jan 2027 DFARS cliff and the 10 Jan 2027 truce expiry. The main risks: legal authority and oversight, concentration in a few firms, budgets dependent on one-time reconciliation money, and Chinese licensing leverage over Dy/Tb.

### Cited Findings
- Capacity gap: MP Independence ramping toward 3,000 t/yr, 10X commissioning 2028, ~10,000 t/yr combined US capacity at full build-out. USAR Stillwater Phase 1a targets 600 t/yr. JL MAG targets 60,000 t/yr by 2027. Lynas made 8 t of Dy/Tb in Q1 2026. The IEA estimates ~$60B of investment is needed by 2035 for non-China rare earth and magnet supply. — [TechTimes, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- China holds ~85–90% of global rare earth refining. The US and Europe together hold ~6–7% of oxide separation capacity. — [TechTimes, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328654/20261006/congress-moves-break-china-rare-earth-grip-before-november-deadline.htm)
- Governance risk: no public disclosure of safeguards to keep federal ownership stakes from influencing permitting, regulation or contracting. ### Inferences
- **Effectiveness:** strongest at the **magnet and oxide** stages, where MP has a floor and offtake and Vulcan and USAR are funded. Weakest at **heavy rare earth separation and metallization**, where DoW is turning to foreign offtakes (Serra Verde) and EXIM LOIs (REAlloys).
- **Consistency risk:**
  - Most instruments rest on executive discretion: EO 14241 waivers, DoW contracts, the CHIPS reallocation, and EXIM financing for Vault.
  - The 10-year contracts (MP floor to ~2035, offtake 10 years from 10X COD) are binding obligations that extend beyond the 2028 election. Their funding still depends on future DoD appropriations and the expiry of OBBBA money (2027–2029).
  - A future administration is more likely to stop new equity deals than to unwind existing contracts.
- **Policy tension:** US negotiators trade rare-earth relief for tariff concessions in the truce, while DoW pays floors premised on a lasting China supply threat. If China eased licensing, NdPr prices would fall and floor payments would rise. The floor therefore functions as a fiscal hedge against China's pricing power.
- **Gaps in the toolkit:** demand stimulus for commercial (non-defense) magnets is missing. 45X does not cover magnets in a way designed for rare earth alloys (needs verification). Heavy rare earth feedstock remains largely foreign. Recycling incentives are only DOE-scale. The FY2027 NDAA Sec. 1801 is unenacted. No codified price-floor or reserve statute exists.
