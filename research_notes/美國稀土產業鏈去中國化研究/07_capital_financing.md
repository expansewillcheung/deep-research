# Capital Flows and Financing into the US Rare Earth Supply Chain (as of Oct 22, 2026)

Status labels used below: **[Disbursed/Closed]** = funded or closed per filing or primary release; **[Definitive]** = signed binding agreement, disbursement milestone-based or not confirmed; **[Conditional/LOI]** = non-binding letter of interest or conditional commitment; **[Reported]** = secondary/press reporting only.

## 1. Government capital: who, how much, on what terms, and what has actually been disbursed

### Takeaway
Since mid-2025 the US government has moved from grants to an "equity + debt + price floor + offtake" toolkit (DoW/OSC, Commerce CHIPS, DFC, EXIM). Headline commitments now exceed ~$20B once Project Vault ($10B EXIM) is included, but most of it is loan capacity, milestone-gated funding, or LOIs. Confirmed cash disbursements are a much smaller share: MP Materials' preferred equity and PPA payments, the Commerce equity issued to USAR, the DFC drawdown at Serra Verde, and some SPV funding.

### Cited Findings

- The floor runs from Q4 2025 for 10 years. — [Mining Weekly / Lynas coverage, Jan 2026](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/lynas-ceo-says-government-policies-are-improving-rare-earths-market-2026-01-21)

### Inferences
- Rough confirmed cash out the door to US-linked rare earth projects by Oct 2026 is likely low single-digit billions:
  - MP's $400M preferred plus about $60M of PPA payments in H1 2026;
  - DFC's first draw(s) to Serra Verde;
  - DoW's SPV contribution, if funded at closing.
  Against this sit headline commitments above $20B (Vault $10B, OSC loans about $1.9B, Commerce $1.6B for USAR, Serra Verde SPV $1.55B, DFC $565M, plus EXIM LOIs above $1.3B). The gap between commitments and disbursements is the key caveat for the report.
- The equity-plus-warrants template (MP → Vulcan/ReElement → USAR → proposed Tanbreez) is now standard. The government is a material shareholder in at least MP and USAR, and holds warrants in Vulcan and ReElement.
- For USAR, Commerce's equity is underwater (entry $17.17 vs about $12.5 in mid-October 2026). Fiscal exposure through equity is real and politically salient.

### Gaps
- Exact disbursed amounts were not found for: the $150M DoW loan to MP (drawn?), the $620M Vulcan loan, the $725M Energy Fuels and $500M Phoenix OSC loans, the USAR $277M direct funding, the DoW $750M SPV contribution, and total DFC drawdowns. None of these appeared in the primary sources reviewed.
- Project Vault: no data on actual purchases or drawdowns, loan tenor or pricing, or announcement date (sources imply Feb 2026, with Mayer Brown commentary in March 2026).
- DOE LPO rare earth loans: none found in this research.
- DFC Ukraine reconstruction fund rare earth investments: not researched (no sources retrieved).
- IRA §45X value for rare earths: not verified in this session. My unverified recollection is that §45X gives a 10% credit on critical-mineral production costs, and that OBBBA added a phase-down after 2030. The report writer should treat this as unconfirmed.
- MP–Maaden JV: closing and funding status unconfirmed.

## 2. Strategic corporate capital (OEMs, trading houses, foreign strategics)

### Takeaway
OEM capital mostly arrives as offtakes with milestone-based prepayments (Apple–MP), not equity. Allied state-backed buyers (Japanese consortium at Lynas, Saudi Maaden with MP/DoW) anchor $110/kg-style floors. Commodity traders (Traxys) and banks are starting to fund working capital.

- **Apple–MP (July 2025):** a $500M multi-year commitment for recycled-material magnets from Fort Worth (Independence), with shipments from 2027. ### Inferences
- OEMs (Apple, and reportedly GM) prefer prepay-and-offtake structures that carry no balance-sheet equity risk. Government floors, not OEM capital, are the main de-risking tool.

### Gaps
- No primary-sourced detail was gathered in this session on the GM, Ford or Stellantis rare earth offtakes (e.g., GM–MP, GM–Vacuumschmelze/eVAC, GM–Noveon), on Sojitz/JOGMEC's 2026 equity in Lynas, or on Korean (POSCO International) magnet offtakes. These need coverage from another workstream or follow-up.

## 3. Private and financial capital (banks, PE/VC, SPACs, public equity and converts)

### Takeaway
Private capital has been abundant since mid-2025, once government floors and equity stakes de-risked the sector:
- MP raised more than $700M in equity;
- Energy Fuels raised $700M in converts;
- USAR raised a $1.5B PIPE;
- VC rounds of $40–75M+ for Phoenix Tailings and Cyclic Materials;
- JPMorgan's SRI ($230B financed or facilitated in year one, across all sectors).
Most of this money follows state-guided de-risking rather than taking stand-alone commodity risk.

## 4. Total capital required vs committed

### Takeaway
Estimates differ by an order of magnitude depending on scope:
- IEA: about $60B outside China over a decade for magnet rare earths;
- William Blair: $360–450B globally by 2030 for a fully integrated chain.
US-relevant commitments to date (about $20–25B headline, including Vault) cover a meaningful share of the IEA-type magnet-chain need, but far less of the broader estimate. Commentators increasingly argue that know-how, permitting and demand, not money, are the binding constraints.

### Cited Findings
- IEA: about $60B of investment needed outside China over the next decade for magnet rare earths, public and private. Refining is nearly half and magnet manufacturing about one-third. — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/rare-earth-elements/executive-summary)
- William Blair: a fully integrated supply chain needs $360–450B of global investment by 2030 (broad scope). — [William Blair](https://www.williamblair.com/Insights/The-Multi-Billion-Dollar-Price-Tag-of-US-Rare-Earth-Independence)
- Federal commitments in June 2026 alone were about $2.9B, with about $1.4B of company capital. — [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/washingtons-five-week-rare-earths-funding-surge-how-the-us-is-rebuilding-a-supply-chain-outside-china/)
- Scale gap: the US makes ≤1,000 t/y of NdFeB magnets, against 10,000–15,000 t/y in Japan. — [Indoneo](https://www.indoneo.com/capital/us-rare-earth-exports-asia-magnet-supply-chain/)
- Counter-view: "capital cannot buy 40 years of process expertise." The bottleneck is know-how, ahead of China's Nov 10, 2026 export-control deadline. — [TechTimes, Oct 2 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328392/20261002/rare-earth-magnets-chinas-november-10-deadline-us-gap-money-cant-close.htm)
- China accounts for 91% of refined rare earths and up to 94% of magnets (JPMorgan Center for Geopolitics). — [JPMorgan CfG](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/center-for-geopolitics/insights/jpmc-cfg-minerals)

### Inferences
- Counting only the equity, loans and SPV funding that most directly target magnet-chain projects (excluding Vault and LOIs) gives about $8–10B committed. Against the IEA's roughly $60B ex-China magnet-chain figure (global, not US-only), this suggests early-stage but material coverage. This is my rough tally; it double-counts nothing knowingly, but it is not an audited figure.

## 5. Cost of capital, price floors, Chinese price risk, and project economics

### Takeaway
The $110/kg NdPr floor (MP, Lynas, and the Japanese–Lynas offtake) has become the de facto Western price reference. It turns merchant commodity risk into quasi-contracted cash flow, which enables low-cost debt such as 12–15-year DFC/EXIM tenors and OSC loans.
- 2026 tested it from both sides. NdPr rose from about $74/kg (Dec 2025) to about $120/kg (mid-2026), shrinking PPA payments. Then ex-China spot fell to about $95/kg in August 2026 because of oversupply, below the floor again.
- That raises taxpayer cost for floor-protected producers and leaves unprotected juniors exposed.

### Cited Findings
- S&P Global: NdPr rose from about $74/kg in Dec 2025 to about $120/kg by mid-2026, driven by supply-security concerns. — [S&P Global NdPr Market Report, Aug 5 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/content/dam/spglobal/global-assets/en/special-reports/critical-mineral-market-reports/Market%20Report%20-%20NdPr%20-%2008052026.pdf)
- Benchmark (Aug 21, 2026):
  - Prices: ex-China NdPr oxide fell to $95/kg CIF Europe and North America (−7.3% from Aug 6 to 20), about 14% below the $110 floor. China domestic PrNd was RMB 721,500/t (about $107.5/kg), at a premium to ex-China prices.
  - Cause: ex-China supply chasing limited non-Chinese demand. Benchmark expects oversupply to persist until US and EU magnet capacity grows.
  - Effect: floor-protected MP and Lynas are insulated, and downstream buyers get sub-floor feed.
  — [Benchmark Source](https://source.benchmarkminerals.com/article/ex-china-ndpr-prices-slip-below-110-kg-price-floor-as-oversupply-builds)
- SMM China PrNd oxide was RMB 738,000–740,000/t, stable, on Oct 21, 2026. — [SMM](https://www.metal.com/rare-earth-oxides/201102250162)
- S&P assessed NdPr at $125/kg CIF North America on Mar 31, 2026. — [Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/chinas-export-cap-shifts-rare-earth-margin-toward-producers)
- China's April 2025 export controls remain in force. The October 2025 expansion covering overseas producers and midstream tech is suspended until Nov 10, 2026. — [Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/chinas-export-cap-shifts-rare-earth-margin-toward-producers)
- Fiscal cost of the floor: FAS estimated about $300M/yr at $60/kg. Actual MP PPA receipts were $42.3M in Q1 2026 and $17.6M in Q2 2026. — [FAS](https://fas.org/publication/unpacking-dod-and-mp-partnership/); [MP Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001801368/000180136826000047/mpmcq22026er.htm)
- Offtakes as financing tools:
  - The Serra Verde SPV uses a 15-year take-or-pay with price floors on Dy/Tb, and it is funded by DoW equity plus a borrowing-base bank revolver. — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/usa-rare-earth-secures-1-55-billion-capitalization-for-government-backed-serra-verde-rare-earth-spv/)
  - The MP 10X offtake (100% of output to DoW for 10 years) underwrote the $1B bank financing. — [MP release](https://mpmaterials.com/news/mp-materials-announces-transformational-public-private-partnership-with-the-department-of-defense-to-accelerate-u-s-rare-earth-magnet-independence/)
- Long-tenor concessional debt: DFC gave Serra Verde 12 years; EXIM LOIs offer 15 years (Aclara, Critical Metals). EXIM requires sponsors to be "well-capitalized with sufficient equity from strategic investors." — [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/us-dfc-backs-serra-verde-with-565m-for-rare-earths-expansion-2026-02-06); [MINING.COM](https://www.mining.com/critical-metals-in-line-for-120m-us-loan-to-fund-greenland-rare-earth-project/)

### Inferences
- At $95/kg spot against a $110 floor, the floor adds about 16% to realized NdPr revenue for protected producers. For marginal Western projects with high opex, that is likely the difference between negative and positive EBITDA. Projects without floors (most juniors) face Chinese price-suppression risk and will struggle to raise non-government debt.
- The floor is becoming bifurcated, with protected "national champions" and unprotected juniors. Pressure for floors to be extended to more producers (Serra Verde already has one) is likely to grow.

### Gaps
- No public IRR sensitivity modeling (floor vs no floor) was found for specific projects. Serra Verde floor levels and Vault purchase prices were not disclosed. No information was found on political-risk insurance or offtake guarantees beyond DFC/EXIM.

## 6. Investor sentiment and equity performance (2025–Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
Rare earth equities had a large policy-driven rally from mid-2025 into mid-2026 that enabled record raises. Since roughly June 2026 they have de-rated hard: MP went from about $65 (late May) to about $39.5 (Oct 21), and USAR trades below the government's $17.17 entry.
